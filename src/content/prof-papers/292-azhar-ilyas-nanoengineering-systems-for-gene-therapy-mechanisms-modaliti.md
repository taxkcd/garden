## Paper overview

This review discusses how nanotechnology enhances gene therapy by improving delivery, stability, and safety of genome editing tools like CRISPR. It covers various nanomaterial carriers, next-generation gene editing systems, challenges like endosomal escape, and future directions including AI-assisted design and clinical translation.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Gene therapy faces challenges such as inefficient delivery of genetic material, instability of cargo, off-target effects, immune reactions, and limitations of viral vectors including safety risks and cargo capacity.

**Why it matters:** Overcoming these challenges is critical to realize the full therapeutic potential of gene editing for treating genetic disorders, cancer, infectious diseases, and enabling personalized medicine.

**Key contributions:**

- Comprehensive overview of nanomaterial-based delivery platforms for gene editing.
- Discussion of next-generation CRISPR variants and their unique delivery challenges.
- Analysis of endosomal escape mechanisms and strategies to improve intracellular release.
- Comparison of polymer- and DNA-based carriers for CRISPR delivery.
- Insights into RNA-based therapeutics including siRNA, miRNA, and antisense oligonucleotides.
