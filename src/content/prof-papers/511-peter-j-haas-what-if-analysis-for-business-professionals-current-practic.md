## Paper overview

This paper studies how business professionals use what-if analysis (WIA) to make data-driven decisions. It finds that while WIA is essential, current tools are often rudimentary and not well suited for non-technical users. The authors conducted interviews and task-based studies with 22 business professionals to understand their practices, challenges, and needs. They implemented a visual analytics prototype with representative WIA techniques and found these improved decision-making speed and confidence. The paper offers design recommendations for better business analytics tools.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Existing what-if analysis research focuses on data scientists and analysts with technical expertise, overlooking business professionals who use WIA through non-technical means. There is limited understanding of how business professionals perform WIA, the tools they use, challenges they face, and how advanced WIA techniques can support them.

**Why it matters:** Business professionals regularly make data-driven decisions but often lack formal technical training and rely on rudimentary tools like spreadsheets. They face challenges such as limited availability of data analysts, inefficient collaboration, and inadequate tool support, which hinder effective decision-making. Improving WIA tools for this large and influential group can enhance business outcomes and efficiency.

**Key contributions:**

- An interview study identifying WIA techniques, tools, and challenges faced by business professionals in data-driven decision-making.
- A task-based study evaluating business professionals’ practical use and perceptions of advanced WIA techniques implemented in a visual analytics prototype.
- Design recommendations for future business analytics systems to better support business professionals in advanced analytics.
