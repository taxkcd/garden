- **Year:** 2021

## Paper overview

This paper describes the PATHS program, an NSF-funded scholarship and support ecosystem at Colorado School of Mines designed to recruit, retain, and support academically talented, low-income, and underrepresented students in computer science. The program provides financial aid, community building, mentoring, outreach, and academic and career counseling, resulting in high retention and graduation rates and increased diversity in the CS student body.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Low-income and underrepresented students face significant barriers to enrolling in, persisting through, and graduating from rigorous computer science programs, leading to underrepresentation in the computing workforce.

**Why it matters:** The demand for skilled STEM professionals, especially in computer science, is growing rapidly. Increasing diversity and inclusion in CS education is critical to meet workforce needs and to provide equitable opportunities for historically marginalized groups.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of a successful S-STEM scholarship program targeting low-income, underrepresented students in CS at a rigorous engineering university.
- Implementation of a cohort model that integrates academic, social, and financial supports.
- Demonstration of high retention (93.9%) and academic success (mean GPA 3.4) among PATHS scholars.
- Evidence that PATHS scholars have increased positive attitudes toward computing, growth mindset, and professional engagement compared to national cohorts.
- A replicable ecosystem model that supports recruitment, retention, and career preparation for diverse CS students.
