# 822 · High-Sensitivity Sensor Based on Diametrical Graphene Strip Plasma-Induced Transparency

- **Year:** 2023

## Paper overview

This paper presents a design and analysis of a sensor based on plasma-induced transparency (PIT) using two parallel graphene strips. The sensor operates in the terahertz frequency range and achieves tunable transparency windows by adjusting graphene's chemical potential and geometrical parameters. The design offers high sensitivity to changes in the surrounding medium, making it promising for applications in terahertz modulators, optical switches, and ultrasensitive sensors.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Achieving tunable and highly sensitive plasma-induced transparency (PIT) in the terahertz band using graphene metamaterials, overcoming limitations of metallic materials such as high propagation losses and fabrication complexity.

**Why it matters:** PIT devices have important applications in optical modulators, switches, and sensors, but conventional metallic structures suffer from high losses and limited tunability. Graphene offers superior optical properties and electrical tunability, enabling better performance and practical applications in terahertz technologies.

**Key contributions:**

- Proposed a simple graphene metamaterial structure with two parallel strips to achieve PIT via bright–bright mode coupling.
- Demonstrated tunability of the PIT window by adjusting graphene chemical potential and carrier mobility.
- Validated the design with FDTD simulations and Lorentzian oscillation coupling model showing good agreement.
- Achieved high modulation depths of up to 96.14% in the PIT window.
- Reported a high sensitivity of 1.25 THz/RIU to refractive index changes in the surrounding medium.

**Data:** No external data needed; simulate transmission spectra based on the paper's described parameters and equations.
