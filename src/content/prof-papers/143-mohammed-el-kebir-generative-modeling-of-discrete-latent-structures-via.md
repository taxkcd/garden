## Paper overview

This paper introduces GReinSS, a novel reinforcement learning framework that models and infers complex discrete latent structures from indirect observations. Unlike traditional methods that struggle with large combinatorial spaces or produce artificial latent states, GReinSS dynamically adjusts rewards to learn distributions over true mechanistic latent states, enabling accurate reconstruction of graphs, sets, and RNA isoforms from noisy or partial data.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Many scientific and practical problems require inferring unobserved mechanistic latent states (such as graphs, sets, or sequences) from indirect observations. Classical methods like expectation maximization do not scale well to combinatorially large latent spaces, and deep learning approaches often learn artificial latent states that do not correspond to true mechanistic states. There is a need for scalable, principled methods that can model and infer true discrete latent states from indirect data.

**Why it matters:** Accurately inferring mechanistic latent states is crucial in fields like biology (e.g., tumor phylogenetics, RNA isoform reconstruction), chemistry, and network science. Existing methods either fail to scale or do not recover true latent states, limiting scientific understanding and practical applications such as cancer research and gene expression analysis.

**Key contributions:**

- Formulation of a general probabilistic learning and inference framework for discrete latent states from indirect observations.
- Development of GReinSS, which uses dynamic reward scaling in policy gradients to maximize data likelihood over latent states.
- Theoretical grounding connecting policy-gradient training with maximum likelihood estimation in combinatorial discrete spaces.
- Introduction of an optimal off-policy sampling theory to improve training efficiency.
- Extensive empirical evaluation on simulated graph and set inference tasks demonstrating superior accuracy and scalability.
