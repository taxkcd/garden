## Paper overview

This paper explores how large language models (LLMs) can be trained to perform deductive reasoning more correctly and efficiently by learning from the A* search algorithm. The authors show that by fine-tuning LLMs on search traces generated by A* and using reinforcement learning with A*-informed rewards, smaller Llama models can significantly improve their reasoning accuracy and efficiency on theorem proving tasks in natural language.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Large language models often produce incorrect or inefficient reasoning chains when performing deductive reasoning in natural language. Existing training methods focus on final answer correctness but do not ensure intermediate inference steps are correct or efficient.

**Why it matters:** Incorrect or redundant reasoning steps reduce interpretability and trust in LLMs' proofs and limit scalability to complex problems. Improving both correctness and efficiency of reasoning chains is critical for practical and scientific advances in AI reasoning.

**Key contributions:**

- Framing natural language deductive reasoning as a search problem guided by A* search over hypergraphs.
- Proposing a method to verbalize A* search traces including backpointers and inference rules for training LLMs.
- Introducing A*-informed process reward models for reinforcement learning to balance correctness and efficiency.
- Empirically demonstrating that Llama-3.2 models (1B–3B parameters) substantially improve accuracy and efficiency on ProofWriter and DeepRD datasets after A* post-training.
- Analyzing the impact of different heuristics and reward models on the accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
