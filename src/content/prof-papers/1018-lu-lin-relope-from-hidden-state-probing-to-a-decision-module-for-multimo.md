# 1018 · ReLope: From Hidden-State Probing to a Decision Module for Multimodal LLM Routing

- **Year:** 2026

## Paper overview

This paper addresses how to efficiently route queries between small and large multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by predicting when the smaller model's answer is correct. It finds that predicting correctness from hidden states is harder when images are involved compared to captions. The authors propose two methods: Attention Probe, which learns to better combine hidden states, and ReLope, which trains a dedicated decision layer inside the MLLM to improve routing decisions. ReLope achieves the best performance across multiple benchmarks, improving accuracy-cost trade-offs with minimal overhead.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to effectively predict the correctness of answers generated by small multimodal large language models to decide when to escalate queries to larger, more expensive models, especially given the challenge that correctness is harder to predict from hidden states when images are involved.

**Why it matters:** Efficient routing between small and large models balances computational cost and performance, which is critical for deploying multimodal LLMs in practical applications. Improving correctness prediction enhances reliability and cost-efficiency of AI systems that process multimodal inputs.

**Key contributions:**

- Empirical demonstration that correctness prediction is harder from hidden states with image inputs than caption inputs in multimodal LLMs.
- Attention Probe method that learns to pool frozen token hidden states, narrowing the prediction gap between image and caption inputs.
- ReLope method that adapts a dedicated decision layer with LoRA and KL-regularized bottleneck for improved correctness prediction without altering answer generation.
- Comprehensive evaluation across five multimodal benchmarks and three backbone models showing ReLope achieves the highest correctness prediction AUC and better accuracy-cost trade-offs.
- Analysis showing that gains come from both adaptation and bottleneck regularization, not just parameter count.

### Beginner — Reproduce Image vs Caption Correctness Prediction Gap
*Effort: a weekend, ~8 hours*

You build a simple probe model that predicts answer correctness from frozen hidden states of a multimodal LLM given image and caption inputs. You reproduce the paper's finding that correctness prediction AUC is higher with captions than images, using a small subset of a public multimodal VQA dataset as a stand-in.

**Why it shows you understood the paper:** This project shows you understand the fundamental empirical challenge the paper addresses: that correctness prediction from hidden states is harder with images than captions, a key motivation for ReLope.

**Grounded in:** Empirical demonstration that correctness prediction is harder from hidden states with image inputs than caption inputs in multimodal LLMs.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.11, PyTorch, transformers, scikit-learn, Jupyter Notebook

**Data:** Use a publicly available subset of the A-OKVQA dataset or a similar open VQA dataset with paired images and captions as a substitute for the paper's data.

**Build it:**

1. Load a pretrained multimodal LLM or a vision-language transformer that outputs hidden states for image and caption inputs.
2. Extract frozen hidden states for a sample of question-answer pairs with both image and caption inputs.
3. Train a simple binary classifier probe on the hidden states to predict answer correctness separately for images and captions.
4. Evaluate and compare the AUC of correctness prediction for image versus caption inputs.
5. Document results and reproduce the key finding that caption inputs yield higher probe AUC than image inputs.

**Ships as:** A GitHub repo with code and a Jupyter notebook showing the probe training and evaluation, reproducing the image-caption correctness prediction gap with plots and metrics.

**Stretch goal:** Add visualization of hidden state attention or token pooling to explore why captions yield better correctness prediction.
