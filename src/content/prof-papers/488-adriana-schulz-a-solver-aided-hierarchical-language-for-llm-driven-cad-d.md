## Paper overview

This paper introduces AIDL, a new domain-specific language designed to enable large language models (LLMs) to generate precise and editable CAD designs by offloading complex spatial reasoning to an external geometric constraint solver. AIDL supports hierarchical design, semantic naming, and explicit constraints, improving the quality and editability of CAD programs generated by LLMs compared to existing CAD languages.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Large language models struggle to generate procedural geometry in CAD due to difficulties in spatial reasoning and long-range planning required for complex geometry generation. Existing CAD domain-specific languages (DSLs) do not fully support the needs of LLMs for generating editable and semantically meaningful CAD programs.

**Why it matters:** Enabling LLMs to generate accurate, editable CAD designs can revolutionize design automation by combining the generative power of AI with the precision and flexibility of CAD systems. This can improve manufacturing, design workflows, and enable new applications in computational design.

**Key contributions:**

- Design of AIDL, a solver-aided hierarchical CAD DSL tailored for LLM-driven design.
- Introduction of a recursive constraint solver that efficiently handles hierarchical constraints.
- Demonstration that AIDL outperforms OpenSCAD in generating CAD programs closer to prompts and with better editability.
- AIDL supports semantic naming, explicit constraints, and hierarchical modularity, addressing key LLM limitations.
- A detailed analysis of existing CAD DSLs and their limitations for LLM use, motivating AIDL's design choices.
