# 444 · Whole-genome Comparisons Identify Repeated Regulatory Changes Underlying Convergent Appendage Evolution in Diverse Fish Lineages

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Gill Bejerano

- **Paper:** [Whole-genome Comparisons Identify Repeated Regulatory Changes Underlying Convergent Appendage Evolution in Diverse Fish Lineages](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msad188)
- **Authors:** Heidi I. Chen, Yatish Turakhia, Gill Bejerano, David M. Kingsley
- **Year:** 2023

## Paper overview

This study investigates how repeated changes in fish fin morphology have evolved independently across diverse fish species by comparing whole genomes. The authors identify genomic regions, especially regulatory DNA deletions, that are repeatedly lost in fish species with reduced pelvic and caudal fins. They validate known and novel regulatory elements controlling fin development and demonstrate their functional roles using genome editing in stickleback fish.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Identifying the genomic basis of traits that have evolved repeatedly in wild species, particularly the regulatory changes underlying convergent fin reduction in diverse fish lineages.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the genetic mechanisms behind repeated evolution can reveal evolutionary hotspots and regulatory elements critical for vertebrate appendage development, with implications for evolutionary biology, developmental genetics, and potentially human disease.

**Key contributions:**

- Developed a genome-wide comparative method to identify conserved sequence deletions (pCONDELs) associated with fin reduction across distantly related fish species.
- Confirmed recurrent deletions of the PelA pelvic enhancer of Pitx1, previously known in sticklebacks, also occur in distantly related fish with pelvic reduction.
- Discovered a novel conserved regulatory element (pCONDEL.1189) near the Faf1 gene that acts as a caudal fin enhancer and is recurrently deleted in fin-reduced species.
- Demonstrated that deletion of pCONDEL.1189 or disruption of Faf1 via CRISPR-Cas9 causes ectopic fin ray growth in stickleback, linking this regulatory region to caudal fin development.
- Provided evidence that repeated regulatory deletions near fin development genes underlie convergent fin morphology evolution over 100+ million years.

**Grounded in:** Confirmed recurrent deletions of the PelA pelvic enhancer reduce enhancer activity and are independently lost in multiple pelvic-reduced fish lineages.

**Tech stack:** TypeScript, React, CSS

**Data:** Enhancer activity images and data extracted from the paper's supplementary figures; no raw data files needed.

**Build it:**

3. Add interactive elements to highlight differences in enhancer activity between fin-complete and fin-reduced species.
