# 130 · Understanding Learners’ Problem-Solving Strategies in Concurrent and Parallel Programming: A Game-Based Approach

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Bruce W. Char
- **Institution:** Drexel University
- **Paper:** [Understanding Learners’ Problem-Solving Strategies in Concurrent and Parallel Programming: A Game-Based Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04789)
- **Authors:** Jichen Zhu, Katelyn Alderfer, Brian Smith, Bruce Char, Santiago Ontañón
- **Year:** 2020

## Paper overview

This paper studies how undergraduate computer science students learn concurrent and parallel programming (CPP) concepts through an educational game called Parallel. The game simulates CPP challenges using puzzles that require players to manage multiple threads and synchronization. The study found that playing the game significantly increased students' confidence (self-efficacy) in CPP concepts and identified three main problem-solving strategies students use: trial-and-error, single-threaded, and multithreaded approaches. Higher self-efficacy correlated with more time spent using multithreaded strategies, which reflect better understanding of CPP.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Concurrent and parallel programming is difficult for students to learn due to the conceptual shift from sequential programming, and there is limited research on how students learn CPP concepts and how to scaffold this learning effectively, especially using game-based learning.

**Why it matters:** As modern computing increasingly relies on multi-core processors, understanding CPP is critical for computer science education and research. Improving how students learn CPP can enhance their programming skills and better prepare them for real-world challenges.

**Key contributions:**

- Demonstrated that playing Parallel significantly increases students' self-efficacy in CPP concepts.
- Identified three distinct problem-solving strategies students use in the game: trial-and-error, single-threaded, and multithreaded problem solving.
- Found a positive correlation between higher self-efficacy and time spent in multithreaded problem-solving, indicating deeper understanding of CPP.
- Provided one of the first direct analyses of undergraduate students' problem-solving actions in CPP within a game-based learning context.
