## Paper overview

This paper argues that artificial intelligence (AI), especially generative AI, can significantly benefit public health if community engagement is central to its development and use. It highlights the need for public health professionals and communities to understand AI, co-design AI tools, and establish governance to ensure ethical, equitable, and effective AI applications that do not worsen health disparities.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The slow adoption of emerging AI technologies in public health and the risk that AI applications may exacerbate health inequities and privacy risks if developed without community engagement and ethical oversight.

**Why it matters:** AI has the potential to transform public health but without inclusive design and governance, it may marginalize vulnerable populations, perpetuate biases, and worsen health disparities, undermining public health goals.

**Key contributions:**

- Highlighting the critical role of community engagement in AI adoption for public health.
- Identifying risks of bias and privacy violations in AI applications without proper oversight.
- Proposing actionable steps for education, participatory design, and governance frameworks.
- Providing examples of participatory AI development and ethical governance initiatives.
