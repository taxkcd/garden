## Paper overview

This paper explores how social robots can support family routines by providing contextual reminders tailored to the family's activities, locations, and members. Through co-design sessions with families and a four-day in-home study deploying a mobile robot, the research investigates how robots can be integrated into daily family life to help with tasks like reminding children to brush their teeth or complete homework. The study finds that families appreciate the robot's reminders, especially as a neutral entity that offloads parental nagging, but also highlights challenges such as robot navigation, reminder accuracy, and the robot's limited enforcement ability.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to effectively integrate social robots into everyday family life to support and sustain family routines through contextual reminders that account for timing, participants, locations, and environmental activities.

**Why it matters:** Family routines promote children's well-being and reduce household stress, but sustaining these routines is challenging due to competing responsibilities and forgetfulness. Robots have potential to assist, but prior reminder systems often lack rich contextual awareness, limiting their effectiveness and acceptance in family settings.

**Key contributions:**

- Outlined the design space for integrating robots into shared family routines.
- Designed and prototyped a mobile robot system providing contextual reminders tailored to family needs.
- Conducted an in-home user study revealing how families perceive and engage with robot-facilitated reminders.
- Provided practical design insights and ethical considerations for future family robot designs.
