## Paper overview

This paper studies how to help users stay oriented and comfortable when their viewpoint in extended reality (XR) environments is controlled externally, especially when moving between different scales and immersion levels, such as from a miniature overview to street-level and indoor views. The authors designed and tested three transition techniques that visualize the viewpoint change to reduce disorientation and discomfort, comparing them to a simple fade-to-black baseline. The study found that techniques showing the user's future position and orientation improved spatial recall and user experience during multi-scale transitions.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to perform externally-guided viewpoint transitions in XR that maintain user orientation and comfort when users have limited control and spatial knowledge during transitions across immersion levels, positions, and scales.

**Why it matters:** Externally-guided viewpoint transitions are common in XR presentations and scenarios where a system or presenter controls the user's viewpoint. Without proper transition design, users can experience disorientation, discomfort, and reduced spatial understanding, which detracts from the effectiveness of XR experiences.

**Key contributions:**

- Design and prototyping of three externally-guided multi-scale viewpoint transition techniques informed by prior research.
- Empirical evaluation of these techniques against a fade-to-black baseline in an immersive presentation scenario using objective spatial recall metrics and subjective user experience measures.
- Design implications for XR viewpoint transitions that maintain continuity, support orientation, and minimize discomfort during externally-guided multi-scale changes.
