- **Year:** 2026

## Paper overview

This study surveyed pre-high school (grades 4-8) and high school (grades 9-12) teachers about their experiences using QuanTime and other quantum-related activities to teach foundational quantum concepts. The activities were designed to be easy to implement and fit into existing curricula. Most teachers found the activities engaging for students and manageable to teach without extra preparation. The study highlights the potential of early quantum education to spark student interest and prepare a diverse future workforce in quantum science and technology.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is no standardized approach to teaching quantum concepts in K-12 education in the US, and many teachers feel unprepared to incorporate these topics into their curricula. This study investigates teachers’ perceptions and experiences with QuanTime and other quantum-related activities designed to introduce quantum concepts to K-12 students.

**Why it matters:** Quantum technologies are rapidly advancing and require a future workforce knowledgeable in quantum science. Early exposure to quantum concepts can demystify the subject, increase student interest, and diversify the field. Understanding teacher experiences is critical to effectively integrating quantum literacy into STEM education.

**Key contributions:**

- Provides empirical data on K-12 teachers’ perceptions of quantum education activities.
- Compares responses between pre-high school and high school teachers.
- Evaluates the usability and curricular fit of QuanTime activities.
- Identifies popular QuanTime activities and teacher preferences.
- Highlights teacher confidence and minimal need for extra preparation or support.
