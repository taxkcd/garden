# 065 · A rapid and low‑cost method to fabricate well of the well (WOW) dishes with arbitrary 3D microwell shapes for improved embryo culture

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Audrey K. Bowden
- **Institution:** Vanderbilt University
- **Paper:** [A rapid and low‑cost method to fabricate well of the well (WOW) dishes with arbitrary 3D microwell shapes for improved embryo culture](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70517-0)
- **Authors:** Yunqin Zhao, Taehyung Yoon, Jennifer Miller, Christopher P. Montville, Audrey K. Bowden
- **Year:** 2024

## Paper overview

This study presents a new, affordable, and fast method to create specialized culture dishes called WOW dishes with customizable 3D microwell shapes to improve embryo growth in vitro. The method uses 3D printing and PDMS molding to produce inserts that fit standard IVF dishes, allowing testing of different microwell shapes like hemispherical and pyramidal. Experiments with mouse embryos showed that hemispherical microwells yield better embryo quality than traditional flat dishes and commercial WOW dishes, suggesting potential to improve IVF success rates.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Current in vitro fertilization (IVF) success rates remain below 33%, partly due to limitations in embryo culture methods. Existing WOW dishes improve outcomes but are costly, inflexible in microwell design, and difficult to rapidly prototype for research to optimize microwell shapes and dimensions.

**Why it matters:** Improving embryo culture quality can increase IVF success rates, which is critical for reproductive medicine. Affordable, customizable culture dishes would enable systematic research to identify optimal microwell designs, potentially leading to better clinical outcomes and accessibility.

**Key contributions:**

- Introduced a versatile, low-cost, and rapid method to fabricate WOW dishes with customizable 3D microwell shapes using 3D printing and PDMS molding.
- Demonstrated the ability to produce multiple microwell designs (hemispherical, pyramidal, truncated cone) in a single dish for parallel testing.
- Validated fabrication accuracy and mold reusability with micro-CT and OCT imaging.
- Conducted embryo culture experiments showing hemispherical microwells yield superior blastocyst quality compared to traditional IVF dishes and commercial WOW dishes.
- Showed that improved embryo quality is due to microwell shape rather than PDMS material effects.

**Grounded in:** Demonstrated ability to produce multiple microwell designs (hemispherical, pyramidal, truncated cone) in a single dish for parallel testing.

**Tech stack:** JavaScript, Three.js or similar 3D library, HTML, CSS

**Data:** Microwell shape dimensions and profiles as described in the paper's figures and methods section; no experimental data needed.

**Build it:**

1. Extract microwell shape parameters (dimensions, curvature) from the paper's figures and text.
2. Use a 3D graphics library (e.g., Three.js) to model each microwell shape in 3D.
3. Create an interactive UI to toggle between shapes and display their dimensions.
4. Add annotations or measurements to highlight key geometric differences.
5. Test the visualization in a browser and document how shapes differ.

**Ships as:** A GitHub repo with a web-based 3D visualization of microwell shapes and a README explaining the shapes and their relevance to embryo culture.

**Stretch goal:** Add a feature to simulate PDMS shrinkage effects on microwell dimensions to reflect fabrication accuracy.
