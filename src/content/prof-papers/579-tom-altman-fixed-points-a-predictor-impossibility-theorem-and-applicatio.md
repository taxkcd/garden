## Paper overview

This paper introduces a hierarchy of computational stages defined by machines and their languages, and proves that no effective algorithmic predictor can uniformly predict all languages in this hierarchy. It further defines an aggregate language MIS, showing it belongs to NP but not to P, thus connecting recursion theory with computational complexity.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Whether there exists an effective predictor family capable of uniformly predicting all stage languages generated by an activation hierarchy, and the complexity classification of an associated aggregate language MIS.

**Why it matters:** Understanding limits of prediction in computational hierarchies informs fundamental recursion theory and complexity theory, revealing inherent computational barriers and deepening knowledge of NP versus P distinctions.

**Key contributions:**

- Introduction of an activation hierarchy with stage machines, domains, and languages generated by an activation operator.
- Proof of the Predictor-Impossibility Theorem showing no effective predictor family exists for all stage languages.
- Definition of the aggregate language MIS and establishment of the Slice Theorem linking MIS to stage languages.
- Bridge Theorem connecting polynomial-time decidability of MIS to existence of a predictor family.
- Proof that MIS belongs to NP but not to P, i.e., MIS ∈ NP \ P.
