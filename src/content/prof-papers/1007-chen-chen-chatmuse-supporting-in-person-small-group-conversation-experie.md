## Paper overview

ChatMuse is a mixed reality system that uses an AI agent to help people have better small-group conversations in person. It listens to what people say and how they behave, then gives real-time suggestions to the user wearing the mixed reality headset on what to say or do to improve the conversation. The system aims to reduce awkward moments, help users contribute more, and keep conversations on track by providing private, timely support without interrupting others.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** In-person small-group conversations are cognitively demanding and complex due to real-time interaction, multiple participants, and the need to interpret verbal and nonverbal cues. Existing conversational support systems mostly focus on dyadic (two-person) interactions and do not adequately address the challenges of group conversations, such as turn-taking, participation inequality, and managing multiple social cues.

**Why it matters:** Effective small-group conversations are essential for sharing information, sparking ideas, and building relationships in everyday and professional settings. Improving conversational engagement and reducing friction can enhance collaboration and communication outcomes. Mixed reality headsets offer potential for real-time conversational support, but designing such support for groups remains an open challenge.

**Key contributions:**

- An interactive mixed reality experience that supports user engagement and participation in in-person small-group conversations through private AI-driven information support.
- A pipeline design that captures and interprets both verbal and nonverbal conversational behaviors using a large language model to generate proactive support.
- A mixed-methods evaluation demonstrating the feasibility, utility, and usability of proactive AI conversational support in mixed reality for group conversations.
- Identification of design considerations for proactive support timing, content types, and unobtrusive overlay placement to preserve conversational ownership and nonverbal cue visibility.
