## Paper overview

This paper investigates how different interactive explanation interfaces can help people better understand and verify the reasoning steps produced by large language models (LLMs) when solving math problems. The authors designed three interactive formats that present LLM reasoning in more user-friendly ways compared to traditional text explanations. A user study with 125 participants showed that these interactive formats improved accuracy in detecting errors, reduced verification time, and were preferred by users.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Traditional chain-of-thought (CoT) explanations generated by LLMs are often long, static, and text-heavy, which makes it difficult for users to comprehend the reasoning process and detect errors effectively. There is a lack of understanding about how to design explanation interfaces that improve human verification of LLM reasoning.

**Why it matters:** LLMs are increasingly used in critical applications like education, where users need to trust and verify AI-generated solutions. Poorly designed explanations can increase cognitive load, slow down verification, and lead to over-reliance on incorrect outputs, undermining trust and effective human-AI collaboration.

**Key contributions:**

- Introduction of three interactive explanation formats (iCoT, iPoT, iGraph) that improve interpretability and usability of LLM reasoning.
- A large-scale controlled user study demonstrating that interactive explanations significantly improve verification accuracy and error localization compared to traditional CoT.
- Design implications and recommendations for building interactive, interpretable AI systems that support user trust, error detection, and human-AI collaboration.
