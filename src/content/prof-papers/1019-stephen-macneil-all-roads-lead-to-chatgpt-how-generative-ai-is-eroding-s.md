## Paper overview

This paper explores how the widespread use of generative AI tools like ChatGPT among undergraduate computing students is changing how they interact with peers and learn together. While AI helps with quick answers and debugging, it is reducing direct peer help, causing students to feel isolated and less motivated. The social support and mentorship that normally help students succeed are weakening, which may harm learning communities and students’ sense of belonging.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The study investigates how generative AI affects social interactions, peer learning, and classroom dynamics among computing students, focusing on whether AI use disrupts traditional help-seeking behaviors and learning communities.

**Why it matters:** Social interactions and peer support are crucial for student learning, motivation, and retention, especially for underrepresented groups. If generative AI disrupts these social dynamics, it could negatively impact students’ academic success and sense of belonging.

**Key contributions:**

- Identification that generative AI mediates many peer help-seeking interactions, often replacing direct peer assistance.
- Documentation of students’ feelings of isolation, demotivation, and shame related to AI use.
- Observation that AI use reduces opportunities for mentorship and informal learning of the hidden curriculum.
- Insight into how AI use leads to homogenization of programming styles and diminishes critical discourse.
- Highlighting the erosion of online community spaces like Discord due to AI answering most questions.
