## Paper overview

This paper presents an observational study of 30 users of proof assistants Rocq and Lean, tools used to write mechanized proofs in computer science and mathematics. The study uses contextual inquiry to understand how users interact with these tools in real-world settings, revealing the iterative nature of proof writing, challenges in communicating with the proof assistant, reliance on external resources, and considerations beyond just completing proofs. The findings aim to inform improvements in proof assistant usability.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Proof assistants are powerful but challenging tools for mechanized proof writing. Despite improvements, their usability and alignment with real user workflows remain insufficiently understood, limiting their effectiveness and adoption.

**Why it matters:** Mechanized proofs are increasingly important in computer science and mathematics, but their complexity and difficulty hinder productivity and broader use. Improving proof assistant usability can accelerate research, verification, and education in technical fields.

**Key contributions:**

- Four detailed observations about proof assistant usage: iterative proof development guided by feedback, challenges in precise communication with the prover, use of diverse external resources, and design considerations beyond just completing proofs.
- Concrete examples and scenarios illustrating real-world proof writing practices and challenges.
- Identification of five opportunities to improve proof assistant usability, such as supporting exploratory proving and better managing proof details.
- A rich dataset and codebook from the study made publicly available to support future research.
