## Paper overview

This study investigates how mutations in the RNA polymerase II trigger loop (TL), a highly conserved domain critical for transcription, interact with each other and with surrounding residues to affect enzyme function and evolution. Using deep mutational scanning in yeast, the authors reveal extensive genetic interactions (epistasis) that shape the TL's function and evolutionary constraints, showing that even highly conserved sequences are tightly integrated with their enzymatic context.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Understanding how mutations within the RNA polymerase II trigger loop and its surrounding domains interact to influence enzyme function and evolution, particularly the extent and nature of epistasis within this highly conserved active site.

**Why it matters:** RNA polymerase II is essential for transcription in eukaryotes, and the trigger loop is critical for its catalytic activity and fidelity. Insights into how mutations affect its function and evolution can inform on fundamental transcription mechanisms and potential regulatory or therapeutic targets.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of a comprehensive Pol II trigger loop interaction landscape via deep mutational scanning of >15,000 variants.
- Demonstration of widespread epistasis within the trigger loop and between the trigger loop and adjacent domains.
- Identification that highly conserved TL sequences are often incompatible when placed in a heterologous Pol II context, indicating tight coevolution.
- Classification of mutants into GOF, LOF, and unclassified groups with machine learning models correlating in vivo phenotypes with biochemical activity.
- Mapping of allele-specific genetic interaction networks revealing distinct functional dependencies among mutants with similar phenotypes.
