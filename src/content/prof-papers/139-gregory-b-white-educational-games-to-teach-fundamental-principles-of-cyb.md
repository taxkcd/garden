## Paper overview

This paper presents the development and use of educational games, specifically the Cyber Threat Defender collectible card game and the Community Cybersecurity Game, to teach fundamental cybersecurity principles to students in grades 5-12, especially targeting rural and Title I schools where access to cybersecurity education and knowledgeable teachers is limited. The games are designed to be fun, require no prior cybersecurity knowledge, and provide experiential learning to improve students' understanding and interest in cybersecurity careers.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is a significant lack of cybersecurity education in K-12 schools, particularly in rural and Title I schools, due to a shortage of teachers with cybersecurity backgrounds and limited resources. This gap contributes to the growing shortage of cybersecurity professionals and leaves many students unprepared for cybersecurity challenges.

**Why it matters:** Cybersecurity incidents are increasing globally with high economic costs, and all sectors including home users are vulnerable. Addressing the shortage of cybersecurity professionals and raising awareness from an early age is critical to improving cybersecurity practices and workforce development. Rural and Title I schools are disproportionately underserved, exacerbating inequities in STEM education and career opportunities.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of CTD, a fun, accessible collectible card game that introduces cybersecurity concepts to students aged 11-99.
- Creation of lesson plans enabling teachers without cybersecurity backgrounds to effectively use the games in classrooms.
- Introduction of the Community Cybersecurity Game to teach budgeting and strategic planning for cybersecurity in organizations.
- Empirical evidence showing improved student understanding and behavior regarding cybersecurity principles after gameplay.
- Demonstration of the games' particular effectiveness in rural and Title I schools, addressing educational inequities.
