## Paper overview

This paper presents the BiRCh project, a large-scale, longitudinal, audio-aligned, morphologically and syntactically annotated corpus of bilingual Russian child and child-directed speech collected from families in multiple countries. The corpus is designed to facilitate research on bilingualism, heritage language acquisition, and language attrition by providing richly annotated naturalistic data.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is a lack of large-scale, richly annotated, longitudinal corpora of bilingual child speech, especially for heritage speakers, which limits the ability to study the developmental trajectories and linguistic phenomena in bilingual and heritage language acquisition.

**Why it matters:** Understanding bilingual and heritage language acquisition is crucial for linguistics, language education, and cognitive science. Detailed corpora enable researchers to disentangle complex processes like language transfer, attrition, divergent attainment, and input differences, which are essential for theoretical and applied linguistics.

**Key contributions:**

- Creation of a 1-million-word audio-aligned transcript corpus with 500K words morphologically and syntactically annotated.
- Development of detailed annotation guidelines for child and bilingual speech, including disfluencies, bilingual phenomena, and morphological features.
- Implementation of a multi-stage annotation pipeline balancing manual and automatic methods to achieve high-quality data.
- Introduction of syntactic annotation capturing ambiguity and null elements relevant to heritage language research.
- Provision of sociolinguistic metadata to contextualize linguistic data.
