# 193 · The Teacher’s Dilemma: Balancing Trade-Offs in Programming Education for Emergent Bilingual Students

## Paper overview

This paper studies how K-12 computing teachers select programming languages and instructional materials to best support emergent bilingual (EB) students, who face language barriers in learning programming. It explores the trade-offs teachers make between accessibility, curriculum alignment, and workforce preparation. The study finds that teachers balance fostering exploration with engagement, preparing students for careers without overwhelming them, minimizing cognitive load through accessible programming representations, and scaffolding learning with familiar syntax.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** K-12 computing teachers face complex trade-offs when choosing programming languages and instructional materials for classrooms with emergent bilingual students, balancing language accessibility, curricular standards, and workforce preparation.

**Why it matters:** Emergent bilingual students are the fastest-growing population in K-12 but are underrepresented in computer science courses due to linguistic barriers. Programming languages and materials are predominantly English-based, imposing cognitive load and limiting accessibility, which hinders equitable participation and learning.

**Key contributions:**

- Identification of four key trade-offs teachers consider when selecting programming tools for EB students: balancing cost and engagement for exploration; aligning workforce preparation without overwhelming learners; minimizing extraneous cognitive load through accessible representations; and scaffolding learning with familiar and intuitive syntax.
- Insight into teachers’ prioritization of block-based programming languages (BBPLs) as accessible entry points and text-based programming languages (TBPLs) for career readiness.
- Highlighting the importance of visual aids, color coding, and immediate feedback to reduce cognitive load for EB students.
- Discussion of the tension between accessibility and rigor in programming education for EB students.
- Recommendations for programming environments that support gradual transitions from BBPLs to TBPLs.
