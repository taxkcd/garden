## Paper overview

This study evaluates how reliable software programs generated by agentic AI systems are compared to traditional human-written Linux utility programs. Using a rigorous multi-stage AI-driven development workflow and fuzz testing techniques, the authors found that AI-generated programs can be as reliable or even more reliable than human code, though they may have different failure modes such as infinite loops. The study also shows that human oversight and careful prompting are essential for producing robust AI-generated software, and that AI workflows can facilitate sustainable software maintenance.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to objectively measure and compare the reliability of agentic AI-generated software programs against established human-written code.

**Why it matters:** As agentic AI systems increasingly generate software, it is critical to understand their reliability and security to determine if such AI-generated code can be trusted and integrated into production systems. This impacts software quality, security, and sustainability.

**Key contributions:**

- Evaluation of AI-generated code reliability using a comprehensive, best-practices agentic AI workflow rather than simple prompt-based coding.
- Comparison of AI-generated code against concrete ground truth: release-quality human-written Linux utilities.
- Use of well-accepted fuzz random testing methods (black box and gray box) to objectively measure reliability.
- Demonstration that AI prompts and workflows can serve as executable specifications enabling cost-effective software sustainability and maintenance.
