## Paper overview

This paper explores clinicians' perspectives on the ethical implications of collecting voice data during health consultations to train AI systems that assist with clinical documentation. Using a design fiction approach centered on an informed consent process, the study interviewed 16 physicians to identify potential benefits and risks of voice data collection. The findings highlight concerns about trust, self-censorship, legal risks, and workflow disruptions, emphasizing the need for careful ethical consideration and evaluation criteria for AI-assisted clinical documentation technologies.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The paper addresses the underexplored ethical implications and clinician perspectives on collecting conversational voice data during clinical consultations for training AI models that assist with medical documentation and administrative tasks.

**Why it matters:** Voice data collection in clinical settings raises significant ethical concerns including patient and clinician trust, privacy, legal risks, and potential impacts on communication and care quality. Understanding these concerns is critical for developing AI systems that are ethically sound and acceptable to stakeholders in high-stakes healthcare environments.

**Key contributions:**

- A case study applying design fiction via an informed consent process to explore clinician perspectives on AI-related voice data collection.
- Identification of eight classes of potential risks of voice data collection for AI assistance in clinical documentation, including workflow disruptions, self-censorship, and errors affecting patient services.
- Reflections on the limitations of current ethical frameworks like informed consent and suggestions for expanded evaluation criteria for AI clinical documentation technologies.
