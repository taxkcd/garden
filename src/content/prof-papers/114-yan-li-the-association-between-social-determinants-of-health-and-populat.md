## Paper overview

This study examines how social determinants of health (SDOH) like asthma, kidney disease, smoking, and food assistance impact health outcomes across 29,126 census tracts in 499 U.S. cities. By grouping cities based on population density and political affiliation, the research identifies how these factors differently affect population health, providing insights for tailored local health policies.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Existing studies on the impact of social determinants of health on population health outcomes are mostly at the county or state level, limiting the usefulness for local policymakers who need more granular data at the city or census tract level.

**Why it matters:** Understanding SDOH effects at finer geographic levels enables local governments to make informed, targeted health policy decisions to improve population health, enhance care quality, and reduce health inequities.

**Key contributions:**

- Developed a novel data curation process to create additional SDOH variables at the census tract level.
- Used a unique grouping framework combining population density and political affiliation to analyze city-level health outcomes.
- Demonstrated that the impact of SDOH factors varies across city groups, suggesting differentiated public health policies.
- Provided empirical evidence supporting targeted resource allocation and policy transfer among similar city groups.
