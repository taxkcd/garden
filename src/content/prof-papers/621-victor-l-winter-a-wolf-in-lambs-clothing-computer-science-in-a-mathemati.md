# 621 · A Wolf in Lamb’s Clothing: Computer Science in a Mathematics Course

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Victor L. Winter
- **Institution:** University of Nebraska - Omaha
- **Paper:** [A Wolf in Lamb’s Clothing: Computer Science in a Mathematics Course](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3545945.3569736)
- **Authors:** Michelle Friend, Andrew W. Swift, Betty Love, Victor Winter
- **Year:** 2023

## Paper overview

This paper describes a novel general education mathematics course that integrates computer science programming to teach mathematical concepts through art creation. The course replaces traditional college algebra for non-STEM students, using programming environments like Bricklayer and Processing to engage students in functions, patterns, and computational thinking. The course improved students' attitudes toward math and quantitative literacy while providing a meaningful alternative to standard algebra courses.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Traditional college algebra courses are often viewed as difficult and unengaging, especially for non-STEM students, leading to low interest, confidence, and poor completion rates. There is a need for alternative general education math courses that are more meaningful and engaging, while also introducing computational thinking and programming.

**Why it matters:** College algebra as a required general education math course is a barrier to graduation for many students, especially low-income and minority students. Most students in algebra-heavy courses do not need the content for their majors, and poor experiences with math reduce interest and self-efficacy. Integrating computer science into math education can provide a more engaging, relevant, and effective pathway to quantitative literacy.

**Key contributions:**

- Design and implementation of a general education math course integrating computer science programming and art.
- Use of programming environments (Bricklayer and Processing) to teach functions, patterns, and computational thinking.
- Development of engaging, open-ended assignments (art shows) that promote creativity and mathematical understanding.
- Evidence of improved student attitudes toward math and quantitative literacy.
- Demonstration that such a course can serve as a meaningful alternative to traditional college algebra.
