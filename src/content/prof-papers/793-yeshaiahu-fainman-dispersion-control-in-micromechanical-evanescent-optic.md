## Paper overview

This paper presents a novel type of optical modulator based on micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS) that can control light properties by moving a dielectric slab near a waveguide. Unlike traditional modulators that change material properties, these MEMS modulators change geometry to achieve strong and low-loss modulation with unique control over light dispersion. The authors demonstrate experimentally that these devices can produce unusual effects on group velocity and dispersion, enabling new applications in integrated photonics such as broadband switching, tunable delay lines, and ultrafast pulse shaping.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Existing integrated photonic modulators often face trade-offs between modulation strength, speed, loss, and bandwidth. Traditional modulators rely on material property changes which limit performance, especially for broadband and low-loss applications. MEMS modulators that mechanically alter waveguide geometry offer advantages but suffer from insertion loss due to mode mismatch and lack precise dispersion control.

**Why it matters:** Efficient, low-loss, broadband optical modulators are critical for integrated photonic systems used in telecommunications, sensing, and signal processing. Improving modulation strength and dispersion control while minimizing loss can enable advanced functionalities like broadband switches, true time delay lines, and nonlinear optical process phase matching, which are challenging with current technologies.

**Key contributions:**

- Identification and experimental demonstration of anomalous dispersive modulation in evanescent MEMS modulators, where group index decreases despite positive effective index modulation.
- Development of a MEMS actuator design enabling nanometric control of slab position and curvature to fine-tune modulation and reduce insertion loss.
- Demonstration that slab curvature can be engineered to minimize mode mismatch loss, enabling high modulation strength with very low insertion loss.
- Comprehensive optical characterization across multiple devices showing reproducible modulation and loss behavior consistent with simulations.
- Proposal of new applications leveraging unique dispersion control capabilities, including broadband interferometric switches, tunable delay lines, and ultrafast pulse shaping.
