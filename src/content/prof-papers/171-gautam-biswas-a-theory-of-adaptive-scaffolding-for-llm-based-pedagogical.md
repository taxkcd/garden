## Paper overview

This paper presents a theoretical framework and an implemented system called Inquizzitor, an AI-powered pedagogical agent using large language models (LLMs) to provide adaptive, personalized formative assessment and feedback for middle school Earth Science students. The system integrates cognitive science theories to scaffold student learning dynamically, aiming to improve engagement and understanding in STEM+C education.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Current LLM-based educational agents often lack grounding in established cognitive and learning science theories, limiting their ability to provide adaptive, developmentally appropriate scaffolding that supports critical thinking and metacognitive skills in STEM+C learning environments.

**Why it matters:** Without theoretical grounding, LLM agents risk delivering user-pleasing but shallow answers that can hinder critical thinking and knowledge retention. Effective adaptive scaffolding is essential to support student learning progress, motivation, and persistence, especially in interdisciplinary STEM+C education where cross-domain expertise and critical thinking are vital.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of a theoretical framework integrating ECD, SCT, and ZPD for adaptive scaffolding in LLM-based agents.
- Implementation of Inquizzitor, an LLM-powered formative assessment agent providing real-time, theory-driven feedback.
- A human-AI hybrid intelligence approach combining automated scoring with teacher collaboration and iterative prompt refinement.
- Empirical evaluation of scoring accuracy and feedback faithfulness using real classroom data from 104 middle school students.
- Systematic evaluation of agent utterances for alignment with theoretical constructs (ZPD, self-efficacy, goal setting) and teacher preferences (readability, on-task behavior, scoring consistency).
