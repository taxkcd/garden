- **Year:** 2023

## Paper overview

This paper studies the parallel complexity of Horn-SAT, a P-complete problem considered hard to parallelize. The authors empirically show that for almost all random instances generated by a known model, the depth (parallel time) of a parallel algorithm solving Horn-SAT grows only polylogarithmically with input size, suggesting that most instances are easier to parallelize than worst-case theory predicts.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Horn-SAT is a P-complete problem, implying it is unlikely to have efficient polylogarithmic-time parallel algorithms (NC algorithms). The problem is to understand the practical parallel complexity of Horn-SAT beyond worst-case theoretical hardness.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the practical parallelizability of P-complete problems like Horn-SAT can guide algorithm designers to develop efficient parallel algorithms for real-world instances, challenging the pessimistic view from worst-case complexity theory.

**Key contributions:**

- Empirical evidence that the parallel depth of PPUR on random 1-3-Horn-SAT formulas is polylogarithmic in input size for almost all parameter values.
- Identification of a critical parameter region where depth grows linearly, corresponding to a phase transition in satisfiability probability.
- Extension and empirical validation of previous theoretical work on parallel algorithms for Horn-SAT.
- Presentation of a parallel algorithm (PPUR) that is simpler to analyze and closely related to the GP algorithm.
