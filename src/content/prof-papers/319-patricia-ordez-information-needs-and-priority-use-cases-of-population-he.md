## Paper overview

This study investigates the information needs, barriers, facilitators, and priority use cases of population health researchers to improve disaster preparedness for hurricanes and floods. Using interviews and card sorting with 15 researchers, it identifies challenges in collaboration and data adoption, and proposes use cases to guide the design of supportive tools and practices for disaster research.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Population health researchers face significant barriers and information gaps that limit their ability to conduct effective disaster preparedness and recovery research for hurricanes and floods. There is a lack of integrated data sharing, collaboration, and supportive tools tailored to their needs.

**Why it matters:** Disasters like hurricanes and floods have major impacts on population health, but research efforts are hindered by fragmented data, collaboration challenges, and technology adoption issues. Improving preparedness can enhance community resilience and health outcomes during and after disasters.

**Key contributions:**

- Identification of seven barriers and six facilitators to collaborative research and data/technology adoption in disaster preparedness.
- Prioritization of 15 key use cases for population health research in hurricanes and floods.
- Development of a mixed-methods approach combining qualitative interviews and quantitative card sorting to understand researcher needs.
- Provision of recommendations for designing supportive tools that address data quality, collaboration, and technology challenges.
- Highlighting the importance of baseline data and community engagement for disaster research.
