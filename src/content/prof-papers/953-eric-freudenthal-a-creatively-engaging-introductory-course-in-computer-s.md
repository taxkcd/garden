## Paper overview

This paper describes the redesign of an introductory computer science course at the University of Texas at El Paso aimed at pre-engineering and STEM students. The course uses media programming and algorithm design to engage students and gently introduce advanced mathematical concepts through programming projects. It emphasizes hands-on learning with graphical programming to build confidence in math and programming, especially for students with limited prior exposure.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Many incoming STEM students have limited programming experience and low confidence in advanced mathematics, which can hinder their motivation and success in STEM majors. Traditional introductory programming courses often fail to engage these students effectively or connect programming with relevant mathematical concepts.

**Why it matters:** Improving engagement and motivation in early STEM education is critical to student retention and success, especially at Hispanic-serving institutions where many students face preparatory math requirements and may lack prior programming exposure. Bridging programming and math can build confidence and better prepare students for STEM careers.

**Key contributions:**

- Redesign of an engaging introductory CS course targeting pre-engineering students at a Hispanic-serving institution.
- Development of a Raster interface that exposes Cartesian coordinates and pixel manipulation to facilitate algorithmic thinking.
- Integration of programming assignments that incrementally explore mathematical concepts such as slope, acceleration, and ballistic motion.
- Demonstration that early exposure to explicit algorithm design and mathematical modeling increases student motivation and understanding.
- Evidence that simple imperative programming exercises can demystify advanced math concepts and improve student confidence.
