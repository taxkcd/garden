- **Authors:** Lauren E. Margulieux, Briana B. Morrison, Adrienne Decker
- **Year:** 2020

## Paper overview

This study tested the use of subgoal labeled worked examples in a semester-long introductory programming course to reduce high withdrawal and failure rates. Subgoal labels break down complex programming procedures into smaller, manageable steps to reduce cognitive load and help novices learn problem-solving more effectively. The intervention improved quiz performance and reduced dropout and failure rates, especially benefiting students at risk of struggling.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Introductory programming courses have high withdrawal and failure rates (30-50%) because students struggle with complex problem-solving procedures and cognitive overload. Traditional instruction often fails to make tacit expert knowledge explicit to novices.

**Why it matters:** Programming is an essential skill with growing demand, but high attrition in introductory courses limits access and success. Improving instructional methods can broaden participation and retention in computing education.

**Key contributions:**

- First classroom-based, semester-long study of subgoal labeled worked examples in programming education.
- Demonstrated that subgoal labels improve initial problem-solving performance on quizzes but not average exam scores.
- Showed subgoal labels reduce variance in exam scores and lower withdrawal and failure rates.
- Identified that subgoal labels are especially effective for students at risk of struggling or dropping out.
- Validated the use of SOLO taxonomy to measure problem-solving understanding in programming.
