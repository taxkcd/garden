# 873 · Assessing Automated Fact-Checking for Medical LLM Responses with Knowledge Graphs

- **Authors:** Shasha Zhou, Mingyu Huang, Jack Cole, Charles Britton, Ming Yin, Jan Wolber, Ke Li
- **Year:** 2025

## Paper overview

This paper presents FAITH, a framework that uses medical knowledge graphs (KGs) to automatically check the factual accuracy of medical responses generated by large language models (LLMs). FAITH breaks down responses into individual medical claims, links these claims to a medical KG, and scores their truthfulness based on the evidence paths found in the KG. The approach does not require reference answers and aligns well with clinician judgments, offering explainable feedback to improve LLM safety in healthcare.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Ensuring the factual accuracy of medical information generated by large language models is critical for safe deployment in healthcare, but existing automated evaluation methods either rely on reference answers, are not robust, or lack explainability.

**Why it matters:** LLMs can produce plausible but incorrect medical information (hallucinations), which risks patient safety, undermines trust, and hinders clinical adoption and regulatory approval of AI in healthcare.

**Key contributions:**

- Developed FAITH, a novel KG-based, unsupervised, reference-free framework for evaluating factuality of medical LLM outputs.
- Demonstrated FAITH's superior correlation with clinician judgments compared to existing NLP metrics and LLM-based evaluators.
- Showed FAITH's robustness to paraphrasing and ability to distinguish LLMs of varying medical capabilities.
- Provided explainable factuality scores pinpointing specific incorrect claims within responses.
- Validated FAITH's utility in improving LLM safety via selective intervention protocols (Reject-to-Answer and Retrieval-Augmented Generation).

### Beginner — Simple Medical Claim Factuality Scorer Using UMLS KG
*Effort: a weekend, ~8 hours*

You build a small Python tool that takes a few manually written medical claims and verifies their factuality by linking entities to the UMLS knowledge graph and scoring truthfulness based on shortest path evidence. This reproduces the core idea of claim decomposition, entity linking, and KG traversal for evidence retrieval on a minimal scale.

**Why it shows you understood the paper:** This project shows you understand the fundamental mechanism of FAITH: decomposing claims, linking to KG nodes, traversing shortest paths, and computing factuality scores based on graph features.

**Grounded in:** FAITH decomposes LLM-generated medical responses into atomic claims, matches entities to nodes in a comprehensive medical KG (UMLS), traverses shortest paths in the KG to find evidence, and computes a factuality score based on path length, relation semantics, entity centrality, and relation co-occurrence.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.11, NetworkX, UMLS Metathesaurus (downloaded subset)

**Data:** A small set of manually created medical claims and a local subset of the UMLS knowledge graph downloaded from the UMLS Metathesaurus.

**Build it:**

1. Download a subset of the UMLS KG focusing on a few medical domains (e.g., diseases, symptoms).
2. Write a Python script to parse a few example medical claims into atomic claims (manually done).
3. Implement entity linking by matching claim entities to UMLS concept unique identifiers (CUIs).
4. Use NetworkX to represent the KG and find shortest paths between claim entities and evidence nodes.
5. Compute a simple factuality score based on path length and relation types as described in the paper.
6. Write a README explaining the scoring method and show example claims with their factuality scores.

**Ships as:** A Python repo with scripts to score factuality of a few medical claims using UMLS KG, with documented examples and explanation of the scoring method.

**Stretch goal:** Add a simple visualization of the evidence paths found in the KG for each claim.
