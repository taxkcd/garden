## Paper overview

This study validates a new survey tool called the Critical Reflection and Agency in Computing Index, designed to measure how computing students develop ethical awareness and confidence to act on ethical issues. The research shows that students who take computing ethics courses score higher on some ethical reflection and agency dimensions, but also tend to hold stronger beliefs that technology alone can solve problems, which presents a challenge for ethics education. The validated index helps educators systematically assess and improve ethics education in computing.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is a lack of validated, theoretically grounded tools to quantitatively measure whether computing ethics education effectively develops students' critical reflection and agency in ethical computing practice.

**Why it matters:** Computing technologies deeply impact society, and software developers have growing ethical responsibilities. Without reliable measures, educators cannot assess or improve how ethics education prepares students to handle ethical challenges in computing.

**Key contributions:**

- Provided empirical evidence validating the Critical Reflection and Agency in Computing Index as a reliable and valid tool for measuring ethical development in computing students.
- Demonstrated that computing ethics courses are associated with higher critical reflection and agency on some dimensions, supporting the effectiveness of current ethics education approaches.
- Identified the emergence of a techno-solutionism factor, highlighting a challenge where ethics education may inadvertently reinforce overconfidence in technological fixes.
