- **Year:** 2020

## Paper overview

This study develops and applies a high-throughput screening pipeline called HIGAN to systematically identify the regulatory DNA elements and transcription factors that control the expression of the cancer-associated gene APOBEC3B. The authors find that inflammatory signaling pathways, especially involving NF-kB and AP-1 transcription factors, cooperate at specific enhancer regions to activate APOBEC3B expression in cancer cells. This work provides a detailed map of how gene regulation is orchestrated in cancer and offers potential targets for therapeutic intervention.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Understanding the gene-centered regulatory networks that control oncogene expression, specifically how upstream signaling pathways and transcription factors interact with cis-regulatory elements to drive aberrant expression of APOBEC3B in cancer cells.

**Why it matters:** APOBEC3B is an intrinsic source of cancer hypermutation linked to poor prognosis and drug resistance. Deciphering its regulatory network is crucial for understanding cancer progression and for developing targeted therapies to modulate its expression.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of HIGAN, a comprehensive pipeline integrating cis- and trans-regulatory genomic screens with functional enhancer dissection.
- Identification of 13 intronic and distal cis-regulatory regions essential for APOBEC3B expression.
- Discovery that NF-kB and AP-1 pathways are the dominant trans-acting inputs regulating APOBEC3B.
- Fine-mapping of enhancer regions showing cooperative binding of NF-kB and AP-1 transcription factors.
- Demonstration that combined inhibition of NF-kB and AP-1 pathways significantly reduces APOBEC3B expression.
