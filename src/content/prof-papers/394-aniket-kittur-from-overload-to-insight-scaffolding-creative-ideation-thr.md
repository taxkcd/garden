## Paper overview

This paper addresses the challenge of helping designers and researchers manage and make sense of the overwhelming amount of inspirational ideas available today. The authors propose using AI, specifically large language models, to automatically organize design stimuli into hierarchical structures based on their functional mechanisms, and to provide analogical cues that help users adapt ideas from different domains creatively. A prototype system was developed and tested with researchers, showing that hierarchical organization and analogical cues improve exploration and adaptation of ideas.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Designers and innovators face difficulty in efficiently exploring and adapting a vast and unstructured space of inspirational stimuli due to information overload and lack of effective organization and guidance.

**Why it matters:** Effective ideation and innovation depend on exposure to diverse inspirations, but the sheer volume and unstructured nature of available ideas hinder users from discovering valuable insights and achieving creative breakthroughs.

**Key contributions:**

- Introduction of a novel AI-powered method to automatically organize large sets of unstructured design stimuli into hierarchical functional mechanism trees.
- Incorporation of analogical thinking cues—diverse cross-domain examples, core functional mechanisms, and transfer strategies—to aid creative adaptation of ideas.
- Preliminary user study demonstrating that hierarchical views improve efficient exploration and that analogical cues support creative adaptation.
- Identification of three categories of stimuli valued by users: familiar yet feasible, previously overlooked but relevant, and novel or unexpected ideas.
