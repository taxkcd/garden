# 126 · The Motivated Can Encrypt (Even with PGP)

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Glencora Borradaile

- **Paper:** [The Motivated Can Encrypt (Even with PGP)](https://doi.org/10.2478/popets-2021-0037)
- **Authors:** Glencora Borradaile, Kelsy Kretschmer, Michele Gretes, Alexandria LeClerc
- **Year:** 2021

## Paper overview

This study investigates whether activists who face real privacy threats can overcome the known usability difficulties of PGP email encryption and continue using it long-term. By surveying activists trained in PGP email encryption, the authors find that motivation and technical ease are key factors in sustained use, while higher-risk activism surprisingly correlates with discontinuing PGP use. This is the first long-term, real-world study of PGP adoption among social movement participants.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** PGP email encryption is known to have usability issues that hinder adoption, especially in controlled lab studies with novice users. However, it is unclear if motivated users with concrete privacy threats, such as activists vulnerable to surveillance, can overcome these usability challenges and adopt PGP for long-term secure communication.

**Why it matters:** Activists, journalists, and lawyers face persistent surveillance threats that jeopardize their privacy and safety. Understanding how these at-risk groups adopt secure communication tools like PGP is critical to improving digital security practices and protecting civil liberties.

**Key contributions:**

- First long-term, in-the-wild study of PGP email encryption adoption.
- Identification of key factors influencing sustained PGP use: technical ease and individual motivation or low activism risk.
- Discovery of a negative correlation between activism risk and long-term PGP use, contrary to expectations.
- Use of QCA methodology to analyze small, detailed case data in cybersecurity usability.
- Insights into tailoring digital security training based on user motivation and risk profile.
