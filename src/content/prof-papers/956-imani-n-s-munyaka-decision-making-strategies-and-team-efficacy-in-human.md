- **Year:** 2023

## Paper overview

This paper studies how different decision-making styles and whether teammates are human or AI affect how well teams work together and feel about their performance in a cooperative word-guessing game. The study finds that autocratic decision-making harms team and self-efficacy, especially when the partner is human, and that people behave differently depending on whether their teammate is AI or human.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How do decision-making styles and AI identity disclosure of teammates impact team efficacy, self-efficacy, partner performance perception, decision-making strategies, and game outcomes in multi-agent human-AI teams?

**Why it matters:** As AI systems increasingly collaborate with humans in various domains, understanding how team dynamics and decision-making styles affect collaboration outcomes is critical for designing effective human-AI teams, especially in complex multi-agent settings.

**Key contributions:**

- First study to investigate decision-making styles' impact on subjective social metrics and user behavior in multi-agent (three-player) human-AI teams with partially observable information.
- Demonstrated that autocratic decision-making style negatively impacts team efficacy and self-efficacy, especially when the partner is human.
- Showed that partner identity disclosure influences how participants perceive team efficacy and partner performance, with AI partners rated more favorably under autocratic style.
- Revealed differences in decision-making strategies depending on partner identity and decision-making style, e.g., participants more likely to follow autocratic AI partners than laissez-faire AI partners.
- Provided qualitative insights into participants' collaboration styles, word selection strategies, and perceptions of teammates.
