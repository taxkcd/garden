## Paper overview

This paper argues that AI teammates should not be strictly obedient but empowered with intelligent disobedience, allowing them to autonomously override human instructions when necessary to improve team safety and performance. It introduces a taxonomy of AI autonomy levels and explains how disobedience manifests differently across these levels, using examples like guide dogs and grammar checkers. The paper also discusses ethical, safety, and accountability challenges in designing such AI agents.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Most cooperative AI systems are designed to rigidly obey human instructions, which can be counterproductive or unsafe. The problem is how to expand AI agency to include intelligent disobedience, enabling AI teammates to make autonomous, context-sensitive decisions that may override human commands for better team outcomes.

**Why it matters:** Strict obedience limits the value AI can contribute as teammates. As AI achieves superhuman performance, allowing intelligent disobedience can enhance safety, efficiency, and collaboration. It also raises critical issues about AI autonomy, ethical alignment, trust, and accountability that must be addressed for safe deployment in real-world cooperative settings.

**Key contributions:**

- Proposed a taxonomy of AI autonomy levels from no autonomy to full autonomy with examples.
- Formalized intelligent disobedience as a critical capability with a five-step cognitive process.
- Mapped intelligent disobedience behaviors to each autonomy level with practical examples.
- Highlighted the importance of online value alignment, transparency, and accountability in AI disobedience.
- Outlined initial boundaries and ethical considerations for researching AI agency and disobedience.
