## Paper overview

This paper describes a week-long extracurricular workshop called the "Guild" designed to introduce women and non-binary undergraduate students from non-computing majors to computing concepts. The workshop included hands-on projects, mentorship, and community-building activities. The study found that participants' computing identity—interest, competence, recognition, and sense of belonging—increased significantly after the workshop. Participants felt supported, welcomed, and inspired to consider computing in their academic and career plans.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Women and non-binary individuals remain underrepresented in computing despite decades of efforts to increase diversity. There is a need for effective programs that engage these groups, especially students from non-computing majors, to broaden participation in computing.

**Why it matters:** The gender imbalance in computing contributes to workforce inequities and limits diversity of perspectives and innovation in technology. Increasing participation of women and non-binary students can help create a more equitable and inclusive computing ecosystem.

**Key contributions:**

- Development and deployment of the Guild workshop tailored for women and non-binary non-computing majors.
- Application of computing identity theoretical framework to evaluate impact on participants.
- Mixed-methods evaluation showing significant positive changes in computing identity sub-constructs.
- Detailed description of workshop design, culture, skill development, and community engagement.
- Insights into the role of peer mentors and collaborative projects in fostering belonging and competence.
