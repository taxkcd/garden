## Paper overview

This study explores how students perceive three equitable grading practices (EGPs) used in an introductory computer science course: a simplified EMRN grading scale, a flexible late submission policy, and replacing midterm exams with multiple smaller quizzes. The research finds that students generally appreciate these practices, especially the flexible late policy and quizzes, but have mixed feelings about the EMRN grading scale due to its limited granularity. The study highlights the importance of clear communication and balancing flexibility with time management to support student learning and reduce anxiety.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Traditional grading practices often disproportionately disadvantage students from underrepresented communities by focusing on behaviors like timeliness and effort rather than learning outcomes. There is a need to explore equitable grading practices that promote fairness and inclusivity in assessment.

**Why it matters:** Traditional grading can increase student anxiety, encourage grade-focused behavior over learning, and fail to align with skills valued in the job market. Equitable grading practices aim to better support marginalized students and improve learning outcomes.

**Key contributions:**

- Detailed investigation of student perceptions of three specific equitable grading practices in a large introductory CS course.
- Mixed-methods approach combining surveys and interviews to capture nuanced student experiences.
- Identification of benefits and challenges of EMRN grading, flexible late policies, and quizzes replacing exams.
- Evidence that flexible late policies reduce stress but may encourage procrastination.
- Evidence that multiple smaller quizzes are preferred over high-stakes exams and help reduce test anxiety.
