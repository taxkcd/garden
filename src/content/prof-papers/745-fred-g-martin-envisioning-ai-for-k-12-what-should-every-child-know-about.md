## Paper overview

This paper discusses the importance of teaching artificial intelligence (AI) concepts to K-12 students. It presents a collaborative initiative by AAAI and CSTA to develop national guidelines for AI education in K-12, outlining key AI concepts children should learn at different grade levels. The paper also reviews existing tools and curricula, highlights challenges in AI education, and calls for AI researchers to contribute educational resources.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Despite AI's growing impact on society and its increasing presence in everyday technologies, there is a lack of structured guidance and curriculum for teaching AI concepts to K-12 students. The problem is how to define what AI knowledge is appropriate for children at various grade levels and how to support educators in teaching these concepts effectively.

**Why it matters:** AI technologies increasingly influence daily life and future job markets. Educating children about AI will help create an informed populace, inspire future AI researchers and developers, and ensure ethical and societal considerations are understood early. Without proper education, misconceptions and fears about AI may persist.

**Key contributions:**

- Formation of a joint AAAI and CSTA initiative to create national AI education guidelines for K-12.
- Definition of five thematic 'big ideas' in AI that every K-12 student should learn, organized by grade bands.
- Survey and description of AI tools and resources accessible to K-12 students and educators.
- Emphasis on ethical, societal, and fairness considerations in AI education.
- Call to action for AI researchers to develop accessible demos and educational resources.
