## Paper overview

This paper investigates how intimate partner abusers use technology to financially harm survivors. It analyzes reports from 158 survivors and 16 financial advocates to identify various technical and deceptive attacks that restrict, sabotage, monitor, or exploit survivors' financial resources. The study proposes a taxonomy of these attacks, explores abuser motivations, and suggests design and policy recommendations to protect survivors.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Financial abuse in intimate partner violence (IPV) is prevalent but understudied in the context of technology-enabled attacks. There is a lack of comprehensive understanding of how abusers exploit digital financial systems and technologies to harm survivors financially.

**Why it matters:** Financial abuse severely restricts survivors' independence, making it difficult to leave abusive relationships or recover financially. As financial services increasingly digitize, survivors become more vulnerable to technology-enabled financial harms, which can have long-lasting negative impacts on their well-being.

**Key contributions:**

- Extension and refactoring of Freed et al.’s attack taxonomy to include financial technologies and social engineering in IPV contexts.
- Identification of four abuser motivations underpinning financial attacks: exploit, monitor, restrict, and sabotage survivors' financial stability.
- Concrete design recommendations for financial systems to address intimate threats through adversarial thinking and consentful interactions.
- Call for financial sector awareness and training to detect and respond to financially abusive activities.
