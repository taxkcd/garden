## Paper overview

This paper presents a detailed ten-year self-report case study of Clark Elliott's recovery from severe mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) that caused profound movement and cognitive impairments. After years of no improvement, treatment with neurodevelopmental optometric rehabilitation (retinal stimulation and therapeutic eyeglasses) combined with cognitive restructuring using visual puzzles led to full recovery of movement and cognition within two years. The study highlights the critical role of visual-spatial processing in cognition and movement and suggests retinal stimulation therapies as effective treatments for similar brain injuries.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Severe mTBI can cause debilitating movement and cognitive impairments that are often considered permanent by medical science. There is a lack of effective treatments addressing the visual-spatial processing deficits underlying these symptoms.

**Why it matters:** Millions of people suffer from brain injuries with symptoms similar to those described, often with no hope of recovery. Understanding and treating the visual-spatial cognitive deficits could improve quality of life and restore function for many.

**Key contributions:**

- Detailed symptom descriptions linking movement impairments to visual-spatial cognitive deficits.
- Demonstration that retinal stimulation and cognitive restructuring can reverse long-term mTBI symptoms.
- Clinical evidence from neurodevelopmental optometric tests and qEEG showing brain activity improvements with therapeutic glasses.
- Development of personalized therapeutic eyeglasses prescriptions based on detailed testing.
- Use of visual puzzles to retrain visual-spatial cognition and attention.
