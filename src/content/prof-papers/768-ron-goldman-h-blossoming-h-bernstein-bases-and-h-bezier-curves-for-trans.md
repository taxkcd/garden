## Paper overview

This paper develops a new mathematical framework that generalizes classical Bernstein bases and Bézier curves to a broad class of function spaces called translation invariant (γ1, γ2) spaces. These spaces include polynomials, trigonometric, hyperbolic, and their discrete analogues. The authors introduce the h–γ blossom, a novel tool combining previous blossoming concepts, to define h–γ Bernstein bases and h–γ Bézier curves. They provide recursive algorithms for evaluation, subdivision, degree elevation, and interpolation of these curves, extending classical geometric modeling techniques to more general function spaces.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to extend the classical polynomial-based Bernstein bases and Bézier curves to more general function spaces generated by two continuous, linearly independent functions γ1 and γ2, especially translation invariant (γ1, γ2) spaces, and develop associated computational algorithms and properties.

**Why it matters:** Classical Bernstein and Bézier techniques are fundamental in computer graphics and geometric modeling but are limited to polynomial spaces. Extending these tools to broader function spaces enables modeling and approximation of shapes and curves with richer structures, such as trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, which are important in applications like computer aided geometric design and signal processing.

**Key contributions:**

- Definition and construction of the h–γ blossom for translation invariant (γ1, γ2) spaces.
- Introduction of h–γ Bernstein basis functions and h–γ Bézier curves generalizing classical polynomial schemes.
- Development of recursive evaluation algorithms and subdivision procedures analogous to classical de Casteljau algorithms.
- Proof of shift-invariance and partition of unity properties for h–γ Bernstein bases.
- Formulation of Marsden identities and degree elevation formulas for these generalized bases.
