## Paper overview

This paper critiques the common use of the label "underrepresented minorities" in engineering education, arguing that the term focuses on numerical representation and marginalizes individuals by emphasizing their minority status. The authors propose shifting the language to "Excluded Identities" to highlight systemic exclusion by the engineering education system and to better address the root causes of inequity.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The persistent inequity and exclusion of women, racial/ethnic minorities, persons with disabilities, and other marginalized groups in engineering education, despite decades of efforts to increase diversity.

**Why it matters:** Engineering education has historically been dominated by white, upper-class, able-bodied men, and current diversity efforts have plateaued, failing to create truly inclusive environments. The language used to describe marginalized groups may perpetuate exclusion and stereotype threat, hindering progress toward equity.

**Key contributions:**

- Critique of the term "underrepresented minority" as a tool that may perpetuate oppression and exclusion.
- Identification of how current labels emphasize numerical representation and marginalize personhood.
- Proposal of the term "Excluded Identities" to reframe the conversation toward systemic exclusion.
- Discussion of structural and cultural practices in engineering education that contribute to exclusion.
- Call for open dialogue on language and paradigms to advance equity in engineering education.
