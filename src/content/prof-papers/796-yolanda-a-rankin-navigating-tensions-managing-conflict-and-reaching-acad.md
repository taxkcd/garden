## Paper overview

This paper discusses the challenges and opportunities that arise from academic disagreements and critiques within the Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and Computer Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW) communities. It explores how tensions and conflicts between researchers, often stemming from different methodologies and epistemologies, can be navigated constructively to advance the field. The authors propose fostering healthy dialogue, understanding power dynamics, and creating supportive spaces for critique to improve scholarly collaboration and innovation.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The increasing diversity of methodologies and epistemologies in HCI and CSCW research leads to tensions and conflicts among scholars, which can result in unproductive academic disputes or 'academic beef'. There is a lack of structured, constructive approaches to managing these conflicts and critiques in ways that benefit the field and its members.

**Why it matters:** Unmanaged academic conflicts can cause personal harm, hinder collaboration, and create divisions within the HCI community. Conversely, constructive critique and dialogue can lead to new collaborations, refinement of scholarship, and the emergence of important research areas such as citational justice. Understanding how to navigate these tensions is crucial for the health and progress of the field.

**Key contributions:**

- Identification of the challenges and impacts of academic critique and conflict in HCI and CSCW.
- Discussion of power dynamics influencing who can engage in critique and how it is received.
- Proposal of norms and strategies for healthy, constructive academic dialogue.
- Highlighting the importance of critical scholarship for advancing the field.
- Bringing together a diverse panel of scholars to share experiences and foster community dialogue.
