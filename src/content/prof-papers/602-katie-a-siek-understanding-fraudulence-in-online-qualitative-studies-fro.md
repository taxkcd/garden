## Paper overview

This study explores how qualitative researchers in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) experience and manage fraudulent participants in online studies. Fraudulent participants are those who deceive about their identity or eligibility, which threatens research integrity and researcher well-being. Through interviews with 16 HCI researchers, the study identifies how fraud is detected, its impacts, and strategies to handle it, offering guidance to improve research practices and training.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Fraudulent participation in online qualitative research studies, where participants misrepresent their identity or experiences, poses challenges to data integrity, ethical research conduct, and researcher well-being, especially in the HCI community.

**Why it matters:** Fraudulence threatens the validity of qualitative data, risks misrepresenting marginalized populations, causes emotional and professional strain on researchers, and complicates ethical decisions about participant inclusion and compensation. The rise of online qualitative methods has increased accessibility but also fraud risks, necessitating better understanding and guidelines.

**Key contributions:**

- An empirical understanding of how HCI qualitative researchers experience and respond to fraudulent participation.
- A practical guide for qualitative HCI researchers on identifying and handling fraudulence.
- Recommendations for the HCI community to improve training, mentoring, transparency, and researcher well-being regarding fraudulence.

### Beginner — Fraudulent Participant Heuristics Demo
*Effort: a weekend, ~8 hours*

You build a small React web app that simulates a participant screener form and flags suspicious entries based on heuristics from the paper, such as suspicious names, emails, and inconsistent responses. The app highlights entries with common names, simple/random email formats, or vague answers, demonstrating basic fraud detection cues.

**Why it shows you understood the paper:** This project shows you grasped the paper's key fraud indicators and can operationalize them into a simple interactive tool, reflecting the paper's empirical findings about participant red flags.

**Grounded in:** Fraudulent participants often exhibit suspicious names/emails, inconsistent or vague responses, and behavioral red flags.

**Tech stack:** React, TypeScript, CSS

**Data:** Simulated participant screener responses created manually to reflect suspicious and normal patterns described in the paper.

**Build it:**

1. Set up a React app with a participant screener form including fields for name, email, and open-ended questions.
2. Implement validation logic that flags suspicious names (e.g., common names), simple/random email formats, and vague or inconsistent answers.
3. Display flagged entries with visual warnings and explanations referencing the paper's fraud indicators.
4. Write a README explaining how the heuristics map to the paper's findings.

**Ships as:** An interactive React app demonstrating heuristic fraud detection on participant screener inputs, with documentation linking heuristics to the paper's results.

**Stretch goal:** Add IP address and geolocation simulation to flag participants outside eligibility regions, as per the paper's detection strategies.

### Intermediate — Qualitative Participant Screening Tool with Fraud Detection
*Effort: 1-3 weekends*

You build a full-stack screening tool that collects participant screener data and applies qualitative fraud detection methods inspired by the paper, including IP/geolocation checks, open-ended qualitative questions, and consistency validation. You simulate a dataset of participant responses with both legitimate and fraudulent entries to evaluate detection accuracy against a simple baseline that uses only demographic filters.

**Why it shows you understood the paper:** This project demonstrates you can implement the paper's core fraud detection approach, combining technical checks and qualitative screening, and quantitatively assess its effectiveness, reflecting the paper's practical guide and empirical insights.

**Grounded in:** Researchers employ strategies like careful screening (e.g., IP checks, qualitative screener questions) and report detection of mismatched information and vague responses.

**Tech stack:** TypeScript, React, Node.js, Express, PostgreSQL

**Data:** Simulated participant screener dataset with labeled legitimate and fraudulent entries, designed to reflect patterns described in the paper.

**Build it:**

1. Design a backend API to store participant screener responses including IP address and qualitative answers.
2. Implement frontend forms for participant screening with open-ended questions and demographic fields.
3. Add fraud detection logic combining IP/geolocation checks, qualitative answer consistency, and demographic validation.
4. Create a simulated dataset of participant responses with injected fraudulent patterns based on the paper's descriptions.
5. Evaluate detection accuracy compared to a baseline using only demographic filters and report metrics in the README.

**Ships as:** A deployable full-stack screening tool with fraud detection features and an evaluation report comparing detection methods on simulated data.

**Stretch goal:** Integrate a simple social support feature for researchers to share flagged participant cases, addressing the paper's recommendation for community platforms.
