# 229 · Usability and Engagement Study for a Serious Virtual Reality Game of Lunar Exploration Missions

## At a glance

- **Authors:** Lizhou Cao, Chao Peng, Jeffrey T. Hansberger
- **Year:** 2019

## Paper overview

This paper presents a serious virtual reality (VR) game that immerses players in lunar exploration missions based on the Apollo 16 mission. The study compares the VR version of the game with a non-VR version to evaluate usability and player engagement. Results show that while the VR version requires more time to learn and play, it increases engagement and interest in space science and lunar exploration history.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How does the use of virtual reality technology in a serious game about lunar exploration affect usability and player engagement compared to a traditional non-VR version?

**Why it matters:** VR technologies offer immersive experiences that could enhance learning and motivation in educational games, but their actual impact on usability and engagement, especially for serious games about space science, is not well understood. Understanding this can guide the design of effective educational VR games.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of a serious VR lunar exploration game based on Apollo 16 mission.
- Comparative usability and engagement study between VR and non-VR versions.
- Detailed analysis of user performance across planning, preparing, and driving phases.
- Evidence that VR increases engagement and interest in space science despite longer task completion times.
- Identification of motion sickness as a potential issue in VR serious games.

**Tech stack:** JavaScript, React, CSS, HTML

**Data:** Simulated user interaction timing data collected from the app itself; no external dataset needed.
