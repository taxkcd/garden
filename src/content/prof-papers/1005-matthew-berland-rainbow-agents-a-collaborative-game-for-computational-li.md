## Paper overview

This paper presents Rainbow Agents, a collaborative game designed for museum settings to promote computational literacy, especially among groups underrepresented in computer science. Players program agents to tend a shared garden, encouraging collaboration and understanding of computational concepts through gameplay. The study reports on a beta test showing how players shift from competitive to cooperative and finally collaborative play, highlighting the game's design to support these transitions.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to design collaborative games in informal learning environments like museums that effectively promote computational literacy and engage underrepresented groups in computer science education.

**Why it matters:** Early playful experiences with computer science can foster interest and pathways into the field, but underrepresented groups face structural barriers. Museums offer unique social and interactive settings to provide accessible, collaborative computational learning experiences that traditional classrooms may not.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of Rainbow Agents, a collaborative game promoting computational literacy through agent-based modeling.
- A framework describing how game mechanics afford different goal alignments (competitive, cooperative, collaborative).
- Empirical case study showing players' shifting goal alignments during gameplay in a museum setting.
- Insights into how initial competitive play can facilitate understanding and later collaboration.
- Design considerations for collaborative learning games in informal museum environments.
