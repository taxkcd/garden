## Paper overview

This paper argues that it is impossible to reliably monitor advanced AI systems to predict certain capabilities before they emerge. It analyzes the complexity, unpredictability, and emergent behaviors of AI, showing fundamental limits to human and automated monitoring. The work discusses implications for AI safety and proposes strategies to address these challenges.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The infeasibility of accurately monitoring advanced AI systems to predict the emergence of certain capabilities prior to their manifestation.

**Why it matters:** Advanced AI systems pose safety risks due to their unpredictability, unexplainability, and uncontrollability. Reliable monitoring is crucial for anticipating unsafe impacts and ensuring AI alignment with human values, but inherent limitations challenge this goal.

**Key contributions:**

- Formal definition of AI monitorability and its complexity via power sets of advanced capabilities.
- Comprehensive taxonomy of AI monitoring types covering functional, safety, ethical, environmental, temporal, meta, and lifecycle aspects.
- Identification and analysis of multiple fundamental reasons why advanced AI systems are unmonitorable, including human cognitive limits, emergent behaviors, deception (Treacherous Turn), consciousness, extended mind hypothesis, observer effect, computational irreducibility, and undetectable backdoors.
- Discussion of monitoring challenges specific to AGI and SAI, highlighting the increasing difficulty as AI surpasses human intelligence.
- Proposal of AI observatories as a collaborative platform to improve monitoring capabilities despite inherent limitations.
