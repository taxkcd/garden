## Paper overview

This paper introduces a new method called Oz that stimulates individual photoreceptor cells in the human retina with precise laser light to create colors beyond the natural human color gamut. The authors built a prototype system that tracks eye movements and targets thousands of cones with laser microdoses, enabling the perception of novel, highly saturated colors and imagery. This approach differs fundamentally from traditional color displays by controlling color at the cellular level, opening new possibilities for vision science and color display technology.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Current color display technologies are limited by the spectral sensitivities of the three cone types in the human eye, restricting the range of perceivable colors to the natural human gamut. There is a need for a method to produce novel colors beyond this gamut by directly controlling individual photoreceptor activations.

**Why it matters:** Expanding the range of perceivable colors could revolutionize color display technology and deepen understanding of human color vision and neural processing. It also enables new experimental platforms for vision science and potential applications in vision therapy and augmented reality.

**Key contributions:**

- Introduction of the Oz principle for cell-by-cell photoreceptor stimulation to produce novel colors.
- Development of a prototype system capable of targeting thousands of cones with laser microdoses under fixational eye motion.
- Demonstration of colors beyond the natural human gamut, including a new color named 'olo' perceived as highly saturated blue-green.
- Formal color matching experiments confirming the existence of colors outside the natural gamut.
- Image and video recognition experiments showing subjects can perceive complex imagery rendered with Oz colors.

**Tech stack:** JavaScript, React, CSS

**Data:** Simulated cone mosaic and fractional light leak parameters based on descriptions in the paper; no external dataset required.
