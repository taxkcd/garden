## Paper overview

This paper systematically reviews 153 CHI conference papers from 2020 to 2024 that involve large language models (LLMs). It categorizes the domains where LLMs are applied, the roles LLMs play in HCI research, the types of contributions made, and the limitations and risks authors report. The study aims to understand how LLMs are shaping HCI research and to provide guidance for future research and responsible design.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is limited understanding of how large language models (LLMs) have been adopted and used in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research, particularly at the CHI conference, despite their growing influence.

**Why it matters:** LLMs are transforming HCI research practices and applications, but their rapid adoption raises concerns about validity, reproducibility, ethical implications, and societal impact. Understanding their use is crucial for ensuring scientific rigor and responsible AI development.

**Key contributions:**

- A taxonomy of 10 application domains where LLMs have been applied in CHI research.
- Identification of 5 distinct roles that LLMs play in HCI projects.
- Classification of 29 common limitations and risks acknowledged by authors.
- Insights into the dominance of empirical and artifact contributions in LLM-related HCI research.
- Provision of guiding questions for researchers to assess the validity and appropriateness of LLM-related studies.
