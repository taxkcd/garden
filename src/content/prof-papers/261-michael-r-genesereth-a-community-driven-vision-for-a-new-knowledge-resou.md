## Paper overview

This paper presents a community-driven vision for creating a new, comprehensive, and open knowledge resource for artificial intelligence (AI). Despite advances in AI and existing knowledge bases, there remains a critical lack of widely available, verifiable, and general-purpose knowledge that AI systems can use for reasoning and decision-making. The authors synthesize discussions from a 2025 AAAI workshop involving over 50 researchers to outline the requirements, challenges, and approaches for building such a resource. They emphasize combining curated knowledge with modern technologies, fostering community collaboration, and improving education in knowledge representation to enhance AI's robustness and interpretability.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Current AI systems lack access to a comprehensive, verifiable, and general-purpose knowledge resource that supports human-like reasoning, leading to gaps in knowledge for tasks such as robotic planning and fact-checking. Existing knowledge graphs and large language models either lack expressivity or formal guarantees, limiting their effectiveness in many applications.

**Why it matters:** Without a reliable and expressive knowledge resource, AI systems struggle with knowledge gaps, resulting in reduced accuracy, transparency, and reliability. This hinders progress in critical applications like robotic task planning, detecting false information, and scientific knowledge synthesis. A robust knowledge resource would enable AI to reason more like humans and support a wide range of applications.

**Key contributions:**

- Articulation of the need for a modern, open, and community-driven knowledge resource for AI.
- Identification of limitations in current knowledge resources and large language models.
- Proposal of an open engineering framework for disseminating and using knowledge modules.
- Discussion of methodologies for formalizing foundational and domain-specific knowledge.
- Exploration of the role of automated reasoning and incorporation of context.
