## Paper overview

This study systematically analyzed publicly available bulk RNA sequencing data from postmortem brain samples of multiple sclerosis (MS) patients to identify alternative splicing events that are not detected by traditional gene expression analyses. The authors found that alternative splicing affects distinct biological pathways related to MS progression and brain region differences, highlighting its critical role in MS pathology and potential as a source of biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Multiple sclerosis is a complex autoimmune neurodegenerative disease with unclear biological changes during progression. While bulk RNA sequencing data are widely used for differential gene expression analysis, alternative splicing events—which can significantly alter transcriptomes—are often overlooked, limiting understanding of MS mechanisms.

**Why it matters:** Alternative splicing contributes to transcriptome and proteome diversity and is implicated in many diseases. Identifying splicing changes in MS can reveal novel molecular mechanisms, explain symptom variability, and uncover new biomarkers or drug targets, especially since many splicing events are independent of gene expression changes.

**Key contributions:**

- Comprehensive reanalysis of all publicly available bulk RNA-seq datasets from MS patient brains focusing on alternative splicing.
- Identification of distinct sets of genes exclusively alternatively spliced in MS that differ from differentially expressed genes.
- Discovery of alternative splicing patterns specific to MS subtypes (progressive vs relapsing-remitting), brain regions, tissue types, and cell types.
- Prediction of disrupted protein domains and protein-protein interactions caused by alternative splicing events.
- Validation of known MS-associated splicing events (e.g., IL7R exon 6 skipping) and identification of novel alternatively spliced genes potentially involved in MS pathology.

### Beginner — Reproduce Alternative Splicing vs Differential Expression Overlap Metric
*Effort: a weekend, ~6 hours*

You will download processed gene lists of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and alternatively spliced genes (ASGs) from the paper's referenced datasets and compute the overlap percentage between these two sets. You will visualize the overlap metric as a simple bar chart or Venn diagram to replicate the paper's reported average overlap of 2.19%.

**Why it shows you understood the paper:** This project shows you understand the distinct biological signals captured by alternative splicing analysis compared to differential gene expression, a key insight from the paper.

**Grounded in:** Overlap between differentially expressed genes and alternatively spliced genes was low (average 2.19%), indicating distinct biological signals.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.11, pandas, matplotlib, seaborn

**Data:** Use gene lists of DEGs and ASGs extracted from the paper's supplementary tables or from processed data available in GEO accessions such as GSE111972 or GSE123496.

**Build it:**

1. Download DEG and ASG gene lists from one or two GEO datasets referenced in the paper (e.g., GSE111972, GSE123496).
2. Load the gene lists into Python using pandas.
3. Calculate the overlap percentage between DEGs and ASGs.
4. Create a bar chart or Venn diagram to visualize the overlap.
5. Write a README explaining the metric and how it relates to the paper's findings.

**Ships as:** A GitHub repository containing code to compute and visualize DEG-ASG overlap, with a README linking the result to the paper's key insight about distinct biological signals.

**Stretch goal:** Add overlap calculations for multiple datasets or MS subtypes to compare overlap variation.
