## Paper overview

This paper develops a principled theoretical framework to design noise schedules in diffusion models used for generative tasks. By formulating noise schedule design as an optimal control problem involving the Fisher information of the diffusion process, the authors derive noise schedules that achieve state-of-the-art sampling error bounds. They also provide closed-form parameterized noise schedules that generalize commonly used empirical schedules and demonstrate improved image generation quality on benchmarks.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** How to design noise schedules for diffusion models that minimize sampling error, bridging the gap between heuristic practical schedules and theoretical guarantees.

**Why it matters:** Noise schedules critically influence the quality of data generated by diffusion models, which are widely used in generative AI. Existing theoretical results guarantee optimal error rates only for specific schedules rarely used in practice, leaving a gap between theory and practice.

**Key contributions:**

- Formulation of noise schedule design as an optimal control problem using Fisher information dynamics.
- Proof that the optimal control solution achieves Õ(d/n) sampling error bounds, extending state-of-the-art guarantees to a broad class of noise schedules.
- Derivation of closed-form affine-coupled noise schedules that include and generalize popular exponential and sigmoid schedules.
- Development of a practical algorithm to tune schedule parameters for any computational budget, improving image generation quality.
- Empirical demonstration that the proposed schedules outperform standard heuristic baselines on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet benchmarks.
