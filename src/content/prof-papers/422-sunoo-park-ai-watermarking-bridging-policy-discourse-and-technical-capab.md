## Paper overview

This paper analyzes the policy discussions and legislative efforts around AI-generated content transparency, especially focusing on AI watermarking. It systematically reviews 49 US and EU policy documents to identify how AI watermarking and related content transparency mechanisms are defined, regulated, and aligned with technical capabilities. The study highlights gaps and ambiguities in policy, proposes a taxonomy for legislation, and offers recommendations for future policy development.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** There is a growing need to reliably identify AI-generated content to address challenges like misinformation, fraud, and misattribution. However, policy discourse on AI watermarking and content transparency is fragmented, inconsistent, and often disconnected from technical realities, making it difficult to develop effective regulations.

**Why it matters:** As generative AI becomes widespread, distinguishing AI-generated from human-generated content is critical for security, privacy, and trust. Without clear, effective policies aligned with technical capabilities, harmful uses of AI content may proliferate unchecked, and transparency mechanisms may not be widely adopted.

**Key contributions:**

- First systematic analysis and taxonomy of AI watermarking and content transparency policy discourse.
- Systematization of 43 legislative documents using a six-parameter framework covering scope, use cases, mandates, disclosure mechanisms, requirements, and penalties.
- Identification of 10 key trends in legislation, including ambiguities in definitions, responsibility allocation, and technical feasibility.
- Highlighting disconnects between policy language and technical practice, especially regarding watermarking definitions and detection.
- Compilation of 10 open questions and areas of uncertainty for future policy development.
