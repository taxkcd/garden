# 099 · FKBP51 Modulates Hippocampal Size and Function in Post-translational Regulation of Parkin

## At a glance

- **Professor:** Gavriil Tsechpenakis
- **Institution:** Purdue University
- **Paper:** [FKBP51 Modulates Hippocampal Size and Function in Post-translational Regulation of Parkin](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-022-04167-8)
- **Authors:** Bin Qiu, Zhaohui Zhong, Shawn Righter, Yuxue Xu, Jun Wang, Ran Deng, Chao Wang, Kent E Williams, Yao-Ying Ma, Gavriil Tsechpenakis, Tiebing Liang, Weidong Yong
- **Year:** 2021

## Paper overview

This study investigates the role of FKBP51, a protein linked to stress-related mental illnesses, in brain development and function. Using FKBP51 knockout mice, the research shows that absence of FKBP51 alters hippocampal size and neuronal morphology, particularly increasing dendritic growth and changing microtubule-associated protein levels. FKBP51 negatively regulates Parkin protein, which is involved in neuronal stability, through a post-translational ubiquitin-mediated mechanism. These findings suggest FKBP51's critical role in neuronal development and its potential impact on neurological diseases.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** The function of FKBP51 in neuronal development and hippocampal morphology, and its molecular mechanisms, particularly its interaction with Parkin protein, remain unclear despite associations with stress-related mental illnesses.

**Why it matters:** Understanding FKBP51's role is important because it is linked to psychiatric disorders like depression and PTSD, which involve hippocampal volume changes. Clarifying its molecular mechanisms could lead to new therapeutic strategies for these and other neurological diseases.

**Key contributions:**

- Demonstrated that FKBP51 knockout leads to altered hippocampal size with more elongated CA and DG regions but shorter hippocampal height.
- Showed that FKBP51 deletion causes increased dendritic outgrowth and altered neuronal morphology in primary hippocampal neurons.
- Identified that FKBP51 regulates microtubule-associated proteins (MAP2, βIII-tubulin, Tau) at the protein but not mRNA level.
- Discovered that FKBP51 negatively regulates Parkin protein expression and stability via a dose-dependent and ubiquitin-mediated mechanism.
- Provided evidence of direct interaction between FKBP51 and Parkin, as well as with microtubule-associated proteins.
