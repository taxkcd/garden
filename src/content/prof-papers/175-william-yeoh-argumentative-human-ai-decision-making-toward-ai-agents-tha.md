## Paper overview

This paper proposes a new paradigm where AI agents engage in collaborative, transparent, and contestable reasoning with humans using computational argumentation combined with large language models (LLMs). Instead of AI making decisions for humans, these agents reason with humans by constructing, evaluating, and revising argumentation frameworks that justify decisions, enabling more trustworthy and human-aware AI in complex, high-stakes domains like medicine and scientific peer review.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Traditional computational argumentation requires structured inputs and extensive feature engineering, limiting scalability and flexibility. Large language models excel at processing unstructured text but produce opaque, hard-to-verify reasoning. The problem is how to integrate these approaches to create AI agents that can transparently and interactively reason with humans rather than simply provide opaque decisions.

**Why it matters:** High-stakes decision-making domains such as medicine and scientific peer review require transparent, contestable, and trustworthy reasoning. Current AI systems often act as black boxes, limiting human trust and control. Developing AI agents that reason collaboratively with humans can improve decision quality, preserve human agency, and foster trust.

**Key contributions:**

- A taxonomy of three core argumentation tasks enhanced by LLMs: mining, synthesis, and reasoning.
- Demonstration of how LLMs overcome limitations of traditional argumentation by enabling scalable, flexible extraction and generation of argumentation frameworks.
- Proposal of contestable AI architectures that separate probabilistic argument generation from deterministic evaluation, allowing interactive human revisions.
- Illustration of applications in medicine and scientific peer review where argumentative human-AI decision-making can improve transparency and collaboration.
- Identification of challenges including evaluation metrics for multi-turn interactions, trust calibration, domain alignment, and governance.
