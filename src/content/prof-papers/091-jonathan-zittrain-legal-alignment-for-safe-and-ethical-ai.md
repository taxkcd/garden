## Paper overview

This paper surveys the emerging field of legal alignment, which aims to design AI systems that operate safely and ethically by ensuring they comply with legal rules, principles, and methods. It highlights how law can provide normative guidance and technical tools to improve AI alignment, complementing existing approaches. The paper outlines three core pathways for legal alignment: using legal rules as alignment targets, applying legal reasoning methods to AI decision-making, and leveraging legal concepts as structural blueprints for AI systems. It also discusses motivations, practical implementations, and open research questions in this interdisciplinary field.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Current AI alignment approaches often overlook the role of law in specifying normative values and guiding AI behavior, leading to limitations in ensuring AI systems act safely, ethically, and in accordance with societal interests.

**Why it matters:** AI systems increasingly impact society with significant opportunities and risks. Ensuring AI systems comply with legal norms enhances their legitimacy, safety, and ethical operation, addressing challenges like ambiguity, conflicting values, and public accountability that existing alignment methods struggle with.

**Key contributions:**

- Definition and contextualization of legal alignment as a distinct research field.
- A taxonomy of three core research pathways for integrating law into AI alignment.
- Analysis of institutional legitimacy, structural features of law, and societal motivations for legal alignment.
- Exploration of practical implementations including empirical evaluations, technical design interventions, and governance frameworks.
- Identification of open questions and a research agenda to guide future interdisciplinary work.
