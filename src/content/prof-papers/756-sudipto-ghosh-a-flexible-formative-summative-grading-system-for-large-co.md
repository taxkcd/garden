## Paper overview

This paper presents a new grading system designed for large introductory computer science courses that supports a growth mindset by allowing students to redo formative assignments with flexible deadlines and demonstrate mastery through summative assessments. The system was applied in CS0 and CS1 courses at Colorado State University and showed that most students improved their grades by resubmitting work, with no significant differences across gender or race. Students who experienced this grading system performed better in a subsequent traditionally graded course (CS2) compared to those graded traditionally in CS1.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Traditional grading systems in large CS courses are restrictive, rely on strict deadlines, and use averaging/scaling that penalizes early failures, which hinders students' ability to develop a growth mindset and master course topics at their own pace. These systems also inadequately support diverse student backgrounds and accommodations.

**Why it matters:** A grading system that does not support learning from mistakes or flexible mastery can negatively impact student retention, equity, and success, especially for students from diverse backgrounds. Improving grading to foster a growth mindset can enhance learning outcomes and future course performance.

**Key contributions:**

- Development of a flexible grading system that supports growth mindset and diverse student needs in large CS courses.
- Integration of the grading system with auto-grading tools and existing course infrastructure.
- Empirical evaluation showing significant grade improvements through resubmissions across diverse student groups.
- Demonstration that students graded under this system perform better in subsequent traditionally graded courses.
- Provision of a growth-focused rubric and encouragement of mastery through formative assignments.
