- **Authors:** Nan Zhang, Ya Yan Lu
- **Year:** 2026

## Paper overview

This paper develops a theory connecting the angular radiation pattern of special nonradiating states called bound states in the continuum (BICs) in photonic structures to their topological properties. It shows how the angular structure of the leading radiation encodes the topological charge of a BIC, predicts transitions between different topological charges, and explains how new BIC families emerge when the structure changes. The authors identify a novel BIC with a high topological charge of -5 and demonstrate how their theory can describe bifurcations and transitions of BICs in symmetric photonic slabs.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Understanding and characterizing the topological nature of bound states in the continuum (BICs) in photonic structures, especially how their angular radiation patterns encode topological charges, and how these charges transition and bifurcate under structural variations.

**Why it matters:** BICs with topological properties are fundamental to resonant and structured-light photonics. They enable ultrahigh quality factor resonances, robust optical cavities, and novel light manipulation techniques such as generating optical vortex beams. Understanding their topology aids in designing photonic devices with enhanced performance and tolerance to imperfections.

**Key contributions:**

- Established a direct connection between the angular structure of leading radiation and the topological charge of BICs.
- Identified a nondegenerate at-Γ BIC with a high-order topological charge q = -5 in a C6v dielectric slab without computing nearby resonances.
- Demonstrated that the leading radiation captures topological-charge transitions and bifurcations of BIC families under structural perturbations.
- Provided a unified local framework linking BIC topology, transitions, and bifurcations using angular radiation analysis.
- Validated theoretical predictions with numerical simulations showing ultrahigh quality factors and polarization winding.
