## Paper overview

This study reports a novel laser solid-phase synthesis method to create highly stable single-atom platinum catalysts supported on indium oxide and cobalt oxide nanoparticles dispersed in reduced graphene oxide. These catalysts efficiently convert carbon dioxide into methanol at 300°C with high selectivity and stability, offering a promising approach for green methanol production.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Single-atom catalysts (SACs) have high catalytic efficiency but suffer from poor stability under high-temperature and reducing conditions, limiting their industrial application for thermal catalysis such as CO2 hydrogenation to methanol.

**Why it matters:** Improving the stability and activity of SACs is critical for practical and industrial applications in CO2 conversion to methanol, a valuable chemical and fuel, contributing to carbon neutrality and sustainable energy solutions.

**Key contributions:**

- Demonstration of a laser solid-phase synthesis method to fabricate atom-nanoisland-sea structured SACs with Pt single atoms on In2O3 supported by Co3O4 nanoislands in rGO.
- Creation of abundant oxygen vacancies (up to 70.8%) via laser processing at extremely high temperatures (~2349 K) within microseconds.
- Establishment of strong interactions between Pt single atoms and In2O3, preventing sintering and aggregation under reducing conditions.
- Comprehensive characterization of catalyst morphology, chemical states, and vacancy defects using advanced microscopy and spectroscopy techniques.
- In situ DRIFTS and DFT simulations elucidating the CO2 hydrogenation mechanism via the formate pathway facilitated by Pt single atoms and oxygen vacancies.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.11, Jupyter Notebook, matplotlib, numpy, pandas

**Build it:**
