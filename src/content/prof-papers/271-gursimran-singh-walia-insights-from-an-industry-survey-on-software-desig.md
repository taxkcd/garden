## Paper overview

This paper presents findings from a survey of software industry professionals to validate and enhance a taxonomy of common software design errors. The taxonomy, originally developed from academic literature, was updated with industry insights to better reflect real-world software design challenges. The study confirms that many design errors stem from knowledge gaps and organizational influences, and suggests that the taxonomy can be used as a practical tool for inspection, training, and improving software quality.

### Why it matters

**Research problem:** Software design errors significantly impact software quality, maintainability, and cost, yet existing taxonomies based on literature may not fully capture errors encountered in industry. There is a need to validate and update the Software Design Error Taxonomy (SDET) with practical industry insights to improve its relevance and usability.

**Why it matters:** Early detection and prevention of software design errors can reduce faults and improve software quality, which is critical given the complexity and cost of fixing errors later in the development lifecycle. A comprehensive, validated taxonomy can guide software engineers to create more robust designs and improve training.

**Key contributions:**

- Validation of the Software Design Error Taxonomy (SDET) with industry professionals.
- Identification of two additional software design errors: 'Influenced by someone else's software design' and 'Influenced by a previous software design', expanding the taxonomy.
- Demonstration that the taxonomy aligns well with industry experiences, especially highlighting knowledge-based and organizational influence errors.
- Provision of a practical framework (SDET) that can be used as a checklist, inspection guide, and training tool to improve software design quality.
